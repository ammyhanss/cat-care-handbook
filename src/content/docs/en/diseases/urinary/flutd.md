## In one sentence

Lower urinary tract signs are a set of findings, not the name of one disease. The common combination is difficulty urinating, blood in the urine, urinating outside the litter box, very frequent urination, and straining with urine that does not pass freely. There are several causes. The most common is idiopathic cystitis.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Introduction] The abbreviation "feline lower urinary tract disease" (FLUTD) has been in use since the 1980s. It only gathers several causes under one term. It is not a diagnosis, and it does not state the cause. Stopping the workup at this abbreviation misses the disease that actually has to be treated.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Introduction]

Urethral obstruction occurs almost only in male cats, and it is life-threatening.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Introduction] A male cat who repeatedly enters the litter box, strains, and cries, yet passes only a little urine or none at all, needs emergency care the same day. Do not wait until the next day.

## Common signs

Signs from different causes look alike, so the signs themselves cannot decide whether it is cystitis, stones, or infection.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Presenting signs of lower urinary tract diseases] Urinating outside the litter box is common. Before it is treated as a behavior problem, lower urinary tract disease is excluded first. Spraying and house soiling are not the same thing.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Presenting signs of lower urinary tract diseases] Some cats over-groom the hair of the belly, the perineum, and the hind legs, which points to pain. Lethargy and a decreased appetite can also occur. When chronic kidney disease is present at the same time, there may also be weight loss, increased drinking and urination, or gastrointestinal signs.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Presenting signs of lower urinary tract diseases]

Idiopathic cystitis is the most common cause of lower urinary tract signs. It has similarities to interstitial cystitis in humans. Organs outside the bladder are also drawn in, and some cats have other discomfort at the same time. It is described as a systemic problem, not only a disease of the bladder.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Feline idiopathic cystitis] Stones account for 10% to 23% of cases with lower urinary tract signs, and they are also an important cause of obstruction. Bladder stones are sometimes an incidental finding and sometimes cause blood in the urine and difficulty urinating. The two most common are calcium oxalate and struvite. They are usually sterile, and together they account for about 90% of feline stones.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Urolithiasis]

With obstruction, the most common finding is straining in the litter box without producing urine, sometimes with crying. Owners often think it is constipation.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Diagnosis] The physical examination can show dehydration, rapid breathing, a slow heart rate, a low body temperature, a bladder that is distended, firm, and painful, and signs of shock.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Diagnosis]

## When to see a vet immediately

- A male cat straining to urinate and passing almost no urine, or no urine at all. Urethral obstruction is a potentially fatal consequence that any lower urinary tract disease can cause. A cat with lower urinary tract signs is triaged quickly, to confirm whether there is obstruction and whether there is shock and hyperkalemia.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Urethral obstruction][^icatcare-lutd-2025|Triage]
- A female cat who suddenly cannot pass any urine also cannot wait. Obstruction occurs almost entirely in male cats, but "almost" is not "never."[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Introduction]
- Known stones together with pain in the loin, vomiting, or sudden marked discomfort. Ureteral obstruction, whether partial or complete, is handled as an emergency.[^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 2.6 Ureteral Obstructions Require Immediate Care]

## Tests the clinic may use

The history includes the environment and behavior, because the signs are similar and the cause cannot be separated by the history alone. The physical examination includes palpating the bladder and looking at the perineum.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Physical examination] Blood tests rarely diagnose lower urinary tract disease directly, but they can find other disease that changes treatment. When obstruction is suspected, triage comes first.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Laboratory testing][^icatcare-lutd-2025|Triage]

Every cat with lower urinary tract signs has a urinalysis. Urine specific gravity is measured with a refractometer, and it is measured before fluids. A dipstick and a urine sediment examined within one hour are both done. Interpretation takes into account whether the food is dry or wet, how the urine was obtained, and how the sample was stored.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Urinalysis] Idiopathic cystitis has no sensitive, specific, clinically available confirmatory test. The diagnosis is one of exclusion: the number and pattern of episodes, the severity, and the risk factors are considered, other causes are ideally excluded with imaging, and the response to treatment is then watched.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Diagnosis]

Imaging can be very useful. Radiographs show radiopaque stones that are large enough. Ultrasound has already replaced contrast studies in many places, but ultrasound does not show the pelvic urethra.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Radiography][^icatcare-lutd-2025|Ultrasound] In an obstructed cat, radiographs often show a distended bladder first, and they can also find part of the cause. In reports, radiographs found an underlying cause in 30% to 40% of obstructed cats and are the preferred initial images.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Diagnosis] Blood changes during obstruction often include azotemia, hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia, and a change in blood phosphorus. A heart rate slower than 140 beats per minute together with a body temperature below 35.5°C is strongly associated with severe hyperkalemia, with a predictive value over 98%.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Diagnosis]

If stones have already been seen, a blood count and biochemistry are done to look for contributing factors. About 35% of cats with calcium oxalate stones have hypercalcemia.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Investigation] Struvite is first separated into sterile and infection-induced. That depends on bacterial culture of urine or of the stone before an antibiotic is used. Most feline struvite is sterile.[^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 3.1 Prevent Sterile Struvite Uroliths by Feeding Therapeutic Maintenance Foods with Low Magnesium and Phosphorus that Acidify Urine] Crystalluria is not a consistent finding when stones are present.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Urolithiasis]

## Treatment approaches

<!-- nonclaim --> This page does not give doses, dosing intervals, or course lengths. Managing an obstruction is emergency medical care, not steps that can be finished at home.

An obstructed cat is treated for shock and hyperkalemia first, and the urine is then relieved. Intravenous fluids are the fastest way to lower blood potassium, and they are started before catheterization. Analgesia is part of treatment. The urinary catheter is not left on open drainage, because the risk of infection is high. Prophylactic antibiotics are not recommended. Bacteria cultured from the catheter or the urine bag may be only bacteriuria, not infection.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Management of hyperkalaemia][^icatcare-lutd-2025|Appropriate use of antibiotics for cats with urethral obstruction] Before discharge it is best to see that the cat can urinate normally. Analgesia after going home has to be something the owner can actually give, and it should not damage the interaction between the cat and the person.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Discharge post-urethral obstruction management]

Because idiopathic cystitis has many causes, a single food or a single drug is unlikely to work on its own. Multimodal environmental modification has been shown to reduce recurrence of all of the signs, and it is the current standard approach.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Management] Prednisone, pentosan polysulfate, and glycosaminoglycans had no significant benefit; the placebo group and the treated group both improved, which may be related to giving the drug in a treat and to lowering the sense of threat. Analgesia is strongly recommended, because this is a painful disease. By the time the guideline was written, meloxicam, robenacoxib, buprenorphine, gabapentin, pregabalin, and frunevetmab had not been studied in non-obstructive idiopathic cystitis. Amitriptyline can be considered for refractory cats.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Pharmacological interventions]

:::evidence-limited

The analgesic names listed above are directions the guideline considers reasonable. They are not a trial that has already shown which one is more effective for non-obstructive idiopathic cystitis. The stress of giving medicine by mouth also has to be counted.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Pharmacological interventions]

:::

Bacterial infection that has clinical signs is treated according to culture and susceptibility, and analgesia is given at the same time. Bacteriuria without signs is generally not recommended for treatment. If a drug has to be started while culture is pending, the first line is a drug of the amoxicillin or trimethoprim-sulfonamide class. A long-acting injection of the cefovecin type should not be used empirically when there has been no urine culture.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Management]

How stones are managed depends on location, the suspected composition, size, and number. Options include medical dissolution, cystotomy to remove the stones, voiding urohydropropulsion, and less invasive methods such as cystolithotomy, extracorporeal or intracorporeal lithotripsy, and removal with a cystoscope and a basket.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Management] Stones that meet the conditions for struvite should have dissolution attempted, unless the drug or the dissolution diet cannot be given, the stone is not bathed in the altered urine, or obstruction is already present. The conditions used to judge this in cats are moderate radiopacity and a urine pH that is about neutral.[^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 1.1 Struvite Uroliths should be Medically Dissolved] For bladder stones that cause signs, the consensus recommends removal by a less invasive method. Stones that cause no signs, and that do not look likely to cause obstruction, do not have to be removed. Urethral surgery done in order to manage stones is discouraged.[^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 1.2 Urocystoliths Associated with Clinical Signs should be Removed by Minimally Invasive Procedures][^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 1.3 Nonclinical Urocystoliths Unlikely to Cause Urinary Obstruction do not Require Removal][^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 1.6 Urethral Surgery to Manage Urolithiasis is Discouraged]

Calcium oxalate stones are not managed with the dissolution diet used for struvite. Prevention of recurrence is to lower the urine concentration, avoid acidifying the urine, and avoid foods with excessive protein. A fully designed clinical trial of recurrence prevention has not been published.[^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 3.3 To Minimize Calcium Oxalate Urolith Recurrence, Decrease Urine Concentration, Avoid Urine Acidification, and Avoid Diets with Excessive Protein Content]

## Care at home

The purpose of environmental modification is to reduce the chances that the cat feels threatened, and to give the cat a sense of control over resources. Other lower urinary tract diseases also benefit when water is easier to drink and the cat is more willing to urinate often.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Environmental and behavioural considerations for management of lower urinary tract diseases] The litter box needs to be large enough, and easy to enter and leave. The number is the number of cats plus one. They are placed in quiet spots where the cat mainly spends time, at least one on every floor, away from food and water. Most cats prefer a fine, clumping litter. Scoop at least once a day, and preferably twice; empty the whole box every 1 to 2 weeks, wash it with a mild detergent, and rinse it with hot water. When in pain, a cat may link the pain with the litter box. Waiting until the signs have passed and the pain is managed, and then offering a new box and a different litter, is better than forcing a change during the episode.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Litter tray management]

After stones are removed or bypassed, the conditions that formed them are still present, so prevention is needed. Sterile struvite is the most common form in cats. Prevention is a therapeutic maintenance food that is low in magnesium, low in phosphorus, and acidifies the urine. The acidification target in this consensus is a urine pH below 6.5.[^acvim-urolith-2016|Urolith Prevention][^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 3.1 Prevent Sterile Struvite Uroliths by Feeding Therapeutic Maintenance Foods with Low Magnesium and Phosphorus that Acidify Urine] For infection-induced struvite, the first priority is to clear the infection, not to rely on food alone. Urine sediment and pH cannot take the place of bacterial culture.[^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 3.2 Primary Prevention of Infection-Induced Struvite Uroliths is Persistent Elimination of Urinary Tract Infection]

Prevention of calcium oxalate is based mainly on high-moisture food, with a target urine specific gravity below 1.030 in cats. Water can be added to dry food. Do not use foods or drugs that acidify the urine. High-sodium dry food is not a substitute for high-moisture food.[^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 3.3 To Minimize Calcium Oxalate Urolith Recurrence, Decrease Urine Concentration, Avoid Urine Acidification, and Avoid Diets with Excessive Protein Content][^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 3.3a Feeding High-Sodium (>375 mg/100 kcal) Dry Foods should not be a Recommended as a Substitute for High-Moisture Foods]

:::evidence-limited

The increase in urination from high-sodium food is described as short-lived. The prevention advice for calcium oxalate comes from epidemiology and from urine saturation, not from a completed trial of recurrence prevention. Cats with high blood calcium have that hypercalcemia treated first; idiopathic hypercalcemia in cats has no single effective treatment.[^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 3.3a Feeding High-Sodium (>375 mg/100 kcal) Dry Foods should not be a Recommended as a Substitute for High-Moisture Foods][^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 3.3 To Minimize Calcium Oxalate Urolith Recurrence, Decrease Urine Concentration, Avoid Urine Acidification, and Avoid Diets with Excessive Protein Content]

:::

A change in nutrition is unlikely, on its own, to be effective for idiopathic cystitis. The studies usually include small numbers of cats, and changing the food can itself cause stress. Obesity is a risk factor, and weight control can be a long-term goal.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Nutritional modifications]

## Outlook and long-term care

A single episode of idiopathic cystitis usually passes. Recurrence is the long-term problem. Environmental modification is the standard approach for reducing recurrence.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Management][^icatcare-lutd-2025|Environmental and behavioural considerations for management of lower urinary tract diseases]

:::evidence-limited

For obstructed cats managed with traditional indwelling catheterization, survival to discharge is reported as 91% to 94%. In the same set of studies, the recurrence rate at different time points was 11% to 58%, and 21% of the cats were eventually euthanized. This range is wide and cannot be treated as the probability for any one cat. Care after discharge, and making the situation clear to the owner, is thought to be something that may improve the long-term result.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Prognosis]

:::

If stones are only removed and the conditions that formed them are not dealt with, they are expected to form again.[^acvim-urolith-2016|Urolith Prevention]

## Common misunderstandings

- "FLUTD is the diagnosis." It is an umbrella term. Idiopathic cystitis, stones, infection, and obstruction are different diseases, and they are managed differently.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Introduction]
- "Straining is constipation." What is most often seen in an obstructed cat is straining in the litter box with no urine, and owners readily read that as constipation.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Diagnosis]
- "Crystals mean stones, and stones mean surgery." Struvite crystalluria does not appear consistently. Struvite that meets the conditions should have dissolution tried first. Bladder stones that cause no signs, and that do not look likely to obstruct, do not have to be removed.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Urolithiasis][^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 1.1 Struvite Uroliths should be Medically Dissolved][^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 1.3 Nonclinical Urocystoliths Unlikely to Cause Urinary Obstruction do not Require Removal]
- "Blood in the urine means an antibiotic." Idiopathic cystitis is the most common cause, and it is a sterile condition. Bacteriuria without signs is generally not treated. Infection that has signs is treated from the culture.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Feline idiopathic cystitis][^icatcare-lutd-2025|Management]
- "A little prescription food will fix it." A single nutritional intervention is unlikely to work on its own for idiopathic cystitis. Environmental modification is the standard approach that has been shown to reduce recurrence.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Nutritional modifications][^icatcare-lutd-2025|Management]
- "A steroid will clear the cystitis." Prednisone has no significant benefit in this disease.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Pharmacological interventions]

## Questions to ask your vet

- Whether this episode is obstruction, stones, infection, or idiopathic cystitis after those have been excluded. The signs themselves do not separate them.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Presenting signs of lower urinary tract diseases][^icatcare-lutd-2025|Diagnosis]
- If this is a male cat and there is very little urine, whether emergency care is needed now rather than an appointment next week. Obstruction can be fatal.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Introduction][^icatcare-lutd-2025|Triage]
- How the composition of a stone is judged, whether struvite should be dissolved first, or whether calcium oxalate needs to be removed. The prevention diets for the two stones go in opposite directions: struvite is acidified, and calcium oxalate avoids acidification.[^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 1.1 Struvite Uroliths should be Medically Dissolved][^acvim-urolith-2016|Recommendation 3.3 To Minimize Calcium Oxalate Urolith Recurrence, Decrease Urine Concentration, Avoid Urine Acidification, and Avoid Diets with Excessive Protein Content]
- Which of the litter-box number, location, and cleaning frequency needs to change at home. The recommendation is one box per cat plus one more, scooped at least once a day.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Litter tray management]
- Which analgesic to use, and how to give it, so that a cat who is already tense is not threatened further. The stress of giving medicine by mouth is counted as part of treatment.[^icatcare-lutd-2025|Pharmacological interventions]
