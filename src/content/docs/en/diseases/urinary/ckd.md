## In one sentence

Chronic kidney disease is a persistent decline in kidney function, or persistent structural damage. Cats do not have a definition written down as rigidly as the human one. In practice the damage is still required to be persistent, not something that appears only once.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Routine diagnosis of CKD in cats] It is common in older cats. In one estimate from first-opinion practice in the United Kingdom, kidney disease accounted for about 4% of diagnoses and was the seventh most common specific diagnosis; 30% to 40% of cats 10 years and older may be affected.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Introduction] In most cats who have had tissue examined, what is seen is chronic tubulointerstitial inflammation and fibrosis. This is regarded as the end point of several earlier injuries, and a single cause often cannot be found.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Introduction]

## Common signs

Owners are more likely to notice weight loss, drinking more, urinating more, and dehydration without a clear reason. A change in the size of the kidneys on palpation, or high blood pressure, is also a reason for further testing. Unexplained low urine specific gravity, in the range the guidelines use, is below 1.035 to 1.040.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Routine diagnosis of CKD in cats] Apart from age, no other clear risk factor has been confirmed. Poor body weight, increased drinking and urination, high creatinine, dehydration, and low urine specific gravity can mean the disease is already present, and they can also be used to notice who is more likely to develop it later.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Introduction]

Early on there are often no obvious signs outside the kidneys. The 2026 IRIS staging describes stage 2 as usually mild clinical findings or none; stage 3 can range from almost no signs to already having many systemic findings; stage 4 is a higher risk of systemic signs and of a uremic crisis.[^iris-stage-2026|IRIS Staging of CKD (Modified 2026)] Poor appetite, nausea, and vomiting come from uremic toxins. They are a main problem for quality of life, and they leave the cat short of calories and protein.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing inappetence, nausea and vomiting in CKD] Low blood potassium can bring lethargy, poor appetite, constipation, and muscle weakness.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing potassium] Anemia is reported in 30% to 65% of cats with chronic kidney disease.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing anaemia in CKD]

## When to see a vet immediately

- Eating almost nothing, repeated vomiting, or weakness to the point of barely moving. A falling appetite in this disease needs to be dealt with actively. Dehydration, low blood potassium, acidosis, and anemia all make the cat eat even less.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing inappetence, nausea and vomiting in CKD] From stage 3 onward, treating these signs outside the kidneys matters more than focusing only on slowing progression; in stage 4 most cats already have many of these findings.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 3 Feline patients][^iris-tx-2026|Stage 4 Feline patients]
- Sudden loss of clear vision, sudden marked weakness, or changes that look as if the brain or the heart is affected. Organs that hypertension damages include the eyes, the heart, the blood vessels of the brain, and the kidneys.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing hypertension in CKD]
- A cat who was stable and then becomes clearly worse over a short time. Staging applies only to stable chronic kidney disease. When creatinine or SDMA changes sharply over a short time, the cat should not be staged directly. Prerenal and postrenal causes have to be excluded first, including pyelonephritis and ureteral obstruction, which can be looked for with imaging.[^iris-stage-2026|1. Staging of Chronic Kidney Disease][^iris-tx-2026|Stage 1 Feline patients]

Drinking more, urinating more, and a slow loss of weight should be booked for blood and urine tests soon. There is no need to wait until the cat stops eating entirely. A single rise in creatinine or SDMA is not, by itself, a diagnosis.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Routine diagnosis of CKD in cats][^iris-tx-2026|Treatment Recommendation for CKD In Cats (2026)]

## Tests the clinic may use

The combination often used in the clinic is azotemia (creatinine is more suitable than urea for reflecting glomerular filtration rate) plus an inappropriately low urine specific gravity. The 2016 consensus often treats a creatinine above 140 µmol/L (1.6 mg/dL), together with a urine specific gravity below 1.035, when those changes have lasted weeks to months, or when the history already shows that they are persistent, as the practical conditions for diagnosis. Not every affected cat meets them: some cats have structural change, or persistent renal proteinuria, before they are azotemic; a few azotemic cats can still reach a urine specific gravity of 1.035 or higher.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Routine diagnosis of CKD in cats]

Creatinine is affected by muscle mass and hydration, and reference ranges differ between laboratories. When kidney function first declines, creatinine may move only a little.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Routine diagnosis of CKD in cats] SDMA is not affected by muscle mass and is more sensitive to an early decline, but it cannot be used as the only screening test.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Symmetric dimethylarginine (SDMA)] Conditions such as lymphoma can also raise SDMA without a fall in filtration rate, so the two markers are read together.[^iris-stage-2026|Discrepancies between creatinine and SDMA]

When this disease is suspected, the routine data should include the history and physical examination, urine specific gravity, a dipstick, urine sediment, the urine protein-to-creatinine ratio, and, when indicated, bacterial culture, serum biochemistry and electrolytes, a blood count, systolic blood pressure, and renal ultrasound. Thyroid hormone is measured in older cats, because hyperthyroidism changes how the cat is managed.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Routine investigation and staging of CKD in cats]

Staging uses cats who are already diagnosed, adequately hydrated, and stable. Creatinine or SDMA (preferably both) is measured at least twice. Substaging by proteinuria and blood pressure comes after that.[^iris-stage-2026|1. Staging of Chronic Kidney Disease] The 2026 creatinine boundaries for cats are: stage 1 below 140 µmol/L (1.6 mg/dL), stage 2 from 140 to 250 (1.6 to 2.8), stage 3 from 251 to 440 (2.9 to 5.0), and stage 4 above 440 (above 5.0). In the same table, feline SDMA (µg/dL) is, in order, below 18, 18 to 25, 26 to 38, and above 38. An SDMA that stays above 14 µg/dL can be used to diagnose early chronic kidney disease.[^iris-stage-2026|IRIS Staging of CKD (Modified 2026)] When creatinine is still in stage 1 but SDMA stays above 18, stage and treat as stage 2. When creatinine is in stage 2 and SDMA stays above 25, treat as stage 3. When creatinine is in stage 3 and SDMA stays above 38, treat as stage 4. When muscle loss is marked, staging follows SDMA.[^iris-stage-2026|Discrepancies between creatinine and SDMA][^iris-tx-2026|Stage 2 Feline patients]

Proteinuria substaging first excludes prerenal and postrenal causes. Ideally at least two urine samples are used, at least two weeks apart. Feline urine protein-to-creatinine ratio: below 0.2 is non-proteinuric, 0.2 to 0.4 is borderline, and above 0.4 is proteinuric.[^iris-stage-2026|2a. Substaging by Proteinuria] Blood pressure is measured several times after the cat has adapted to the setting. Systolic pressure below 140 mmHg is normal, 140 to 159 is prehypertensive, 160 to 179 is hypertensive, and 180 and above is severely hypertensive. When target-organ damage has not already been caused, persistence is confirmed at separate visits.[^iris-stage-2026|2b. Substaging by Blood pressure]

## Treatment approaches

<!-- nonclaim --> This page does not give doses, dosing intervals, or course lengths. What follows only states which directions the guidelines treat as a starting point for management.

Treatment falls into two kinds: slowing progression, and treating clinical signs so that quality of life is maintained. Stages 1 and 2 usually focus on slowing progression. From stage 3, signs outside the kidneys become common. By stage 4, improving quality of life matters more than slowing progression. Each cat's plan is adjusted according to the response.[^iris-tx-2026|Treatment Recommendation for CKD In Cats (2026)]

A renal prescription diet is the mainstay of treatment. These foods are lower in both protein and phosphorus than ordinary maintenance foods, and they are often paired with adjustments such as a higher calorie density, potassium, alkalinization, and omega-3 fatty acids. A moderate protein restriction is recommended, while weight, muscle, and the calories actually eaten are watched, because a protein restriction that is too strict causes loss of muscle, and calorie needs may increase in cats 13 years and older.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Protein and phosphate restriction] Studies show that this kind of diet can reduce uremic signs and prolong survival.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Protein and phosphate restriction] High phosphorus is associated with progression of the disease, and the improvement in lifespan is thought to come mainly from restricting phosphorus, not from restricting protein alone.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Protein and phosphate restriction] The 2026 treatment recommendation is to consider starting a renal diet in stage 2, while the appetite has not yet worsened and a change is easier, and to feed it in stage 3 and stage 4.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 2 Feline patients][^iris-tx-2026|Stage 3 Feline patients][^iris-tx-2026|Stage 4 Feline patients]

:::evidence-limited

Median survival (days, all causes of death) in three reported studies, ordinary diet versus renal prescription diet, was 264 versus 633, 210 versus 480, and about 730 versus a median not yet reached at the end of the study. The direction agrees. The numbers of days cannot be added together or treated as a guarantee for any one cat.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Table 3]

:::

When diet alone cannot hold blood phosphorus down, an intestinal phosphate binder is added and given with food. No study has directly compared the different binders used in cats. Calcium-containing binders require monitoring of blood calcium, because hypercalcemia occurs occasionally. When a change to a renal diet cannot be made, a binder can be added to ordinary food, but that food contains a lot of phosphorus and the effect is reduced.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Use of phosphate binders] Even when blood phosphorus is already inside the target, a renal diet can still raise blood calcium in some cats. The veterinarian checks blood calcium and, when needed, changes to a food whose phosphorus restriction is less strict.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 1 Feline patients]

Water is available all the time. When illness has caused loss of body fluid, dehydration is corrected with isotonic fluids that contain multiple electrolytes. The route is decided by the veterinarian. Some cats in stage 3, and cats in stage 4, may also need maintenance fluid therapy.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 1 Feline patients][^iris-tx-2026|Stage 3 Feline patients][^iris-tx-2026|Stage 4 Feline patients] Low blood potassium is supplemented with a potassium salt until an effect appears. A renal diet has usually already had potassium added and may still not be enough; late in the disease the potassium is occasionally high instead.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing potassium][^iris-tx-2026|Stage 2 Feline patients]

Treatment is required when systolic pressure stays above 160 mmHg, or when target-organ damage is already present. The goal is to bring it below 160 mmHg, and to avoid lowering it so fast that hypotension results. Reducing the sodium in the food, by itself, has no evidence that it lowers blood pressure. Drug directions include a calcium channel blocker (for example amlodipine) or an angiotensin receptor blocker (telmisartan), combined when needed. These drugs must not be started while the cat is dehydrated. After the cat is stable, blood pressure is measured again at least every three months.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 1 Feline patients] The 2016 consensus writes the prevalence of hypertension associated with chronic kidney disease in first-opinion practice as 19% to 40%, and up to 65% in referral populations. Hypertension has not been shown in cats to be an independent risk factor for progression, but it is associated with the severity of proteinuria, and proteinuria can decrease after blood pressure is controlled.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing hypertension in CKD]

Cats whose urine protein-to-creatinine ratio stays above 0.4 are investigated for a treatable cause and are treated with an inhibitor of the renin-angiotensin system plus a renal diet. Borderline values are followed closely. This class of drug is contraindicated during dehydration.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 1 Feline patients] Benazepril can reduce the degree of proteinuria, and telmisartan also reduced proteinuria in a six-month multicenter study. A benefit in survival time has not been proved.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing proteinuria in CKD]

:::evidence-limited

Borderline proteinuria is associated with progression, so antiproteinuric treatment can be considered. There is currently no evidence that using a drug for borderline proteinuria slows progression.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 1 Feline patients]

:::

When appetite is poor, with nausea, vomiting, and loss of muscle, the drugs the guidelines list include maropitant, ondansetron, oral or transdermal mirtazapine, and capromorelin.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 2 Feline patients] In placebo-controlled trials, cats in stage 2 or stage 3 had less vomiting after two weeks of oral maropitant; after three weeks of oral mirtazapine, vomiting decreased and appetite and body weight increased.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing inappetence, nausea and vomiting in CKD]

:::evidence-limited

The two trials above are short-term. IRIS states that more research is still needed on long-term use of these drugs for uremia-related gastrointestinal problems. Any help to appetite from histamine-receptor blockers and proton pump inhibitors remains anecdotal. Gastric hyperacidity and gastric ulceration have not been established in feline chronic kidney disease.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 2 Feline patients][^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing inappetence, nausea and vomiting in CKD]

:::

Anemia is associated with survival. Intervention studies have not set an optimal hematocrit target. Treatment options include darbepoetin, a hypoxia-inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase inhibitor (for example molidustat), and blood transfusion. Anemia caused by chronic kidney disease is treated when the hematocrit is below 25%, and it is also treated when it stays at 25% to 28%. When the hematocrit is already below 25% in stage 2, it is less likely to be caused by the kidney disease itself, and other causes are looked for first.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 2 Feline patients]

Active vitamin D (calcitriol) has reports of benefit in dogs and people. Low-dose use in feline chronic kidney disease has not shown the same benefit.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Calcitriol therapy]

## Care at home

Each day, look at how much was eaten, whether there was vomiting, body weight, and drinking. Weight and muscle are recorded, because creatinine is misled by muscle mass.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Protein and phosphate restriction][^isfm-ckd-2016|Symmetric dimethylarginine (SDMA)] Fresh water is available at all times. A change to a renal diet is made gradually. When appetite is poor, do not force the change to the point that the cat refuses food completely.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 2 Feline patients][^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing inappetence, nausea and vomiting in CKD] Give only medicine the veterinarian has prescribed. Before blood-pressure drugs and drugs that lower proteinuria are started, the cat must not be dehydrated.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 1 Feline patients]

For cats 7 years and older, International Cat Care, the International Society of Feline Medicine, the American Association of Feline Practitioners, and the American Animal Hospital Association recommend a health examination every six months, including body weight, body condition, and blood pressure; blood tests, biochemistry, and urinalysis at least once a year. Serial creatinine or SDMA plus urine specific gravity helps recognize this disease earlier, or with more certainty.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Routine diagnosis of CKD in cats]

Apart from age, no preventable risk factor has been confirmed. Regular checks are for finding, a little earlier, disease that has already started. They are not a method that has been shown to stop the disease from occurring.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Introduction][^isfm-ckd-2016|Routine diagnosis of CKD in cats]

## Outlook and long-term care

This is a progressive disease. The aim of management is to improve quality of life and, while there is still a chance, to slow progression, especially in stage 2 and stage 3.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Approach to management] Stage is related to prognosis, but median survival differs widely between studies.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Routine investigation and staging of CKD in cats]

:::evidence-limited

In four studies summarized by the 2016 consensus, median survival (days) for stages 2, 3, and 4 was: 1151, 778, and 103; in one study stage 2 had not reached a median by the end of follow-up, stage 3 was about 500, and stage 4 was about 70; 504, 154, and 57; and 490, 263, and 20. These numbers cannot be converted into a life expectancy for one particular cat.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Table 2]

:::

The heavier the proteinuria, the shorter the median survival in one study: about 1000 days when the urine protein-to-creatinine ratio was below 0.2, about 500 days from 0.2 to 0.4, and about 400 days above 0.4. Closely similar results have also been reported elsewhere.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing proteinuria in CKD] After drugs are started, staging is updated from the new blood pressure and urine protein, and the current grade is noted as being on treatment.[^iris-stage-2026|3. Revision of staging and substaging after treatment]

## Common misunderstandings

- "One high creatinine means chronic kidney disease." A single elevation is not a diagnosis. Persistent functional or structural damage is required, and short-term causes such as dehydration and obstruction have to be excluded.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Routine diagnosis of CKD in cats][^iris-tx-2026|Treatment Recommendation for CKD In Cats (2026)]
- "The earlier protein is restricted, the more the kidneys are saved." A moderate protein restriction is mainly to reduce uremic signs. The evidence for slowing progression points more toward phosphorus restriction. A restriction that is too strict causes loss of muscle.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Protein and phosphate restriction]
- "Putting in a little less salt will bring the blood pressure down." There is no evidence that lowering dietary sodium lowers blood pressure. When a drug is needed, the veterinarian chooses it and follows it.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 1 Feline patients]
- "A little protein on the dipstick is enough." The dipstick is not sensitive. Substaging uses the urine protein-to-creatinine ratio, and inflammation and blood in the urine are excluded first.[^iris-stage-2026|2a. Substaging by Proteinuria]
- "Drugs that lower proteinuria will lengthen every cat's life." These drugs can lower proteinuria. A benefit in survival time has not been proved in cats.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing proteinuria in CKD]

## Questions to ask your vet

- What stage this cat is in now, and which proteinuria and blood-pressure substages apply. Staging uses repeated measurements when the cat is stable and adequately hydrated. It cannot rest on a single blood draw.[^iris-stage-2026|1. Staging of Chronic Kidney Disease]
- Whether creatinine and SDMA agree. When muscle is low, SDMA is less likely to be pulled down by muscle mass.[^iris-stage-2026|Discrepancies between creatinine and SDMA][^isfm-ckd-2016|Symmetric dimethylarginine (SDMA)]
- Whether blood phosphorus and blood calcium are currently on target, whether a renal diet has already been started, or whether a phosphate binder is still needed.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 2 Feline patients][^isfm-ckd-2016|Use of phosphate binders]
- Whether appetite and vomiting need a drug. The published trials of drugs that stop vomiting and increase appetite are short-term.[^isfm-ckd-2016|Managing inappetence, nausea and vomiting in CKD][^iris-tx-2026|Stage 2 Feline patients]
- Which items are rechecked at the next visit. After blood pressure is stable, it is reassessed at least every three months; routine checks for older cats are more frequent than that.[^iris-tx-2026|Stage 1 Feline patients][^isfm-ckd-2016|Routine diagnosis of CKD in cats]
