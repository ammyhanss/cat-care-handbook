## In one sentence

Tooth resorption is loss of hard tissue, including dentin, because of odontoclast activity. Resorption of the roots of deciduous teeth is a physiologic event. This page is about pathologic tooth resorption. In veterinary dentistry it is seen most often in domestic cats, especially older cats.[^wsava-dental-2020|Tooth Resorption] The nomenclature of the American Veterinary Dental College divides severity into stages 1 through 5 and, by the radiographic appearance, into types 1 through 3, and it treats this process as one that goes on.[^avdc-nomenclature-2026|Tooth Resorption][^wsava-dental-2020|Classification]

## Common signs

When the lesion has not yet reached the mouth, neither the appearance nor palpation finds anything. An earlier clinical stage can be only inflammation at the gingival margin, or a very small defect of enamel and dentin covered by tissue rich in blood vessels. As it goes further, the crown can disappear in part or completely.[^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings] What an owner may see is less grooming, picking food up and putting it down, pawing at the mouth, hiding, lethargy, thick discharge stuck to the lips, shaking the head, rubbing the mouth on the ground, or grinding the teeth. Without these findings, it cannot be inferred that there is no lesion, and it cannot be inferred that there is no pain. Most cats with this disease show no outward discomfort.[^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings] Dentin exposed on the tooth surface is painful, and it can also cause local infection.[^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings]

What the eye can see is, for the most part, already late disease in which the crown has been reached. A very small finding by eye or with a probe often means the disease has already gone deep, because resorption starts on the root surface and the enamel collapses only after it loses its support.[^wsava-dental-2020|Tooth Resorption][^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings] In one study that used clinical examination plus radiographs and enrolled 228 clinically healthy cats, the tooth affected most often was the mandibular third premolar, and most cats were symmetric on the two sides.[^wsava-dental-2020|Tooth Resorption]

:::evidence-limited

The cellular process of resorption is the part that is better understood. The cause is not clear in most cases. It was once taken to be a disease of modern life, but it is also present in wild cats and in material from the late Middle Ages, which conflicts with that account. Resorption associated with inflammation (periodontal disease or stomatitis) is described as type 1. Resorption of unknown cause is described as type 2. Any trauma can cause resorption on the root surface. Some of it heals, and some of it does not.[^wsava-dental-2020|Tooth Resorption]

:::

## When to see a vet immediately

- About a day with almost no food intake, continuous drooling, or a face that swells suddenly. Pain in the mouth makes a cat stop eating. Secondary findings of this disease include picking food up and putting it down, pawing at the mouth, and hiding.[^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings]
- Bleeding from the mouth that does not stop. After the crown collapses, the defect can expose dentin.[^wsava-dental-2020|Tooth Resorption]

Without these emergencies, but with bad breath, slow eating, or a willingness to eat only soft food, a dental examination should still be arranged. Do not wait for the cat to show obvious pain. Most affected cats have no outward sign of discomfort, and exposed dentin is still painful.[^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings]

## Tests the clinic may use

An examination of the mouth while the cat is awake underestimates the disease. A complete assessment needs probing under anesthesia, tooth mobility, full-mouth dental radiographs, and a record.[^aaha-dental-2019|page 4][^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings] The probe is walked along the gingival margin of every tooth. Intact enamel is smooth; when resorption is present the probe catches. Roughness under the gingiva can be resorption, and it can be calculus. When there is doubt, it is assessed again after the teeth are scaled.[^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings] It can be taken for a broken tooth, for attrition, or for furcation exposure. Telling it apart from furcation exposure often depends on intraoral radiographs.[^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings]

Both staging and the treatment plan need dental radiographs. The guidelines strongly recommend full-mouth images in cats. If cost is limited, the mandibular third premolar can be radiographed as a sentinel tooth, but that is not a complete assessment.[^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings] Type 1 on radiographs is a focal lucency, or lucencies in several places, on the tooth, while the remaining radiodensity, the periodontal ligament space, and the pulp system are still normal: there is destruction, and it has not been replaced by bone. Type 2 has, in at least some areas, narrowing or disappearance of the periodontal ligament space, and decreased radiodensity of part of the tooth: it is replacement resorption. Type 3 is the same tooth showing features of both type 1 and type 2 at once.[^wsava-dental-2020|Classification][^avdc-nomenclature-2026|Types of Resorption Based on Radiographic Appearance]

Staging by severity is also part of this nomenclature. Stage 1 is mild loss of hard tissue (cementum, or cementum and enamel). Stage 2 is loss that reaches the dentin but has not yet entered the pulp cavity. In stage 3 the loss of dentin has reached the pulp cavity, and most of the tooth is still intact. Stage 4 is extensive loss, and most of the tooth has already lost its integrity. Stage 5 leaves only irregular radiopaque remnants, and the gingiva has covered them completely.[^avdc-nomenclature-2026|Stages of Tooth Resorption][^wsava-dental-2020|Classification]

## Treatment approaches

<!-- nonclaim --> This page does not give doses, dosing intervals, or course lengths.

The goal of treatment is to relieve pain and to control infection. The resorption is caused by the affected cat's own cells. Filling the defect cannot stop the resorption, so restoration is not recommended.[^wsava-dental-2020|Treatment options] A resin sealant used for tooth resorption is contraindicated.[^aaha-dental-2019|page 10] The best approach is to extract the affected tooth. For inflammatory resorption, removal of the entire root is confirmed, and radiographs are used to confirm it. Grinding the root away with a bur is discouraged.[^wsava-dental-2020|Treatment options]

When radiographs already show resorption, but it has not yet entered the mouth and there is no bone loss along with it, clinical and radiographic monitoring is an option. Rechecks have to be regular. The interval written in the guidelines is 6 to 12 months, so that surgery, when it is needed, is not put off too late. If follow-up of that kind cannot be done, extraction or crown amputation should be considered.[^wsava-dental-2020|Treatment options]

In type 2, because the root has already been partly or wholly replaced by bone-like tissue, extraction can be very difficult, or even impossible. Crown amputation can be done in that situation, but all of the following have to be met at the same time: the pulp system is not seen on radiographs, the periodontal ligament is not seen, there is no clinical or radiographic lesion of the pulp, there is no clinical or radiographic lesion of the periodontium, and there is no caudal stomatitis. In type 3, the root that is type 2 can be amputated, and the root that is type 1 still has to be extracted.[^wsava-dental-2020|Crown amputation] Dental procedures, including scaling and treatment that goes further, require general anesthesia, an endotracheal tube, monitoring, and physiologic support. So-called anesthesia-free dental cleaning has not been shown to be safer than, or equal to, cleaning above and below the gumline under anesthesia, and for that reason it is not accepted.[^aaha-dental-2019|page 10] Surgical extraction is performed by a practicing veterinarian who has been trained to do it.[^aaha-dental-2019|page 6] Most dental procedures are regarded as clean-contaminated surgery, and systemic antibiotics are usually not needed after extraction.[^aaha-dental-2019|page 8]

## Care at home

Record how fast the cat eats, whether food is put down, whether the mouth is pawed at, body weight, and grooming. These are the secondary findings listed in the guidelines. Their absence also cannot exclude the disease.[^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings] Do not cancel the anesthetic assessment before the procedure because the cat "does not look as if it hurts." An examination while awake underestimates the lesions, and the pain is often not shown.[^aaha-dental-2019|page 4][^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings] Pain relief after the cat goes home, and the recheck, are arranged by the veterinarian. If monitoring is chosen instead of extracting at once, the agreed clinical and radiographic rechecks are kept. When those rechecks cannot be done, the guidelines recommend changing to extraction, or to crown amputation when the conditions are met.[^wsava-dental-2020|Treatment options]

No method has been shown that can prevent this disease. The cause is not clear in most cases, so no home measure is written in the guidelines as able to stop it from occurring.[^wsava-dental-2020|Tooth Resorption] Prevention of periodontal disease (professional care under anesthesia, and effective oral hygiene at home) addresses periodontal disease. Tooth resorption is a different process. Filling the tooth, and sealing the tooth surface, cannot stop it.[^aaha-dental-2019|page 10][^wsava-dental-2020|Treatment options]

## Outlook and long-term care

This process progresses. Once the crown is involved, it becomes easy to see in the clinic, and by then the disease is often no longer early.[^wsava-dental-2020|Tooth Resorption][^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings] Extracting the affected tooth is the best approach. Type 2 that meets every condition can have the crown amputated. If the conditions are not met, it is not an indication for amputation.[^wsava-dental-2020|Treatment options][^wsava-dental-2020|Crown amputation] The same cat often has more than one tooth affected. In the study of 228 clinically healthy cats, the pattern was symmetric in most cats, so when one side is seen, the other side and the mandibular third premolar are both assessed.[^wsava-dental-2020|Tooth Resorption]

## Common misunderstandings

- "If the cat is still eating, the mouth does not hurt." Most affected cats have no outward sign of discomfort. Exposed dentin is painful. A lack of pawing at the mouth, or of refusing food, cannot be used to exclude a lesion.[^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings]
- "There is a small hole in the gum, so watching is enough." A very small clinical finding often means the resorption has already reached the crown. When a change in the crown can be seen, the disease is already late.[^wsava-dental-2020|Tooth Resorption][^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings]
- "Filling it will fix it." Restoration cannot stop odontoclastic resorption, so it is not recommended. A sealant is contraindicated.[^wsava-dental-2020|Treatment options][^aaha-dental-2019|page 10]
- "The teeth can be dealt with properly without anesthesia." Anesthesia-free dental cleaning is not accepted. A complete assessment, and extraction, need general anesthesia and intraoral radiographs.[^aaha-dental-2019|page 10][^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings]
- "This is the same disease as chronic gingivostomatitis." Tooth resorption is resorption of hard tissue. Chronic gingivostomatitis is inflammation of the mucosa. The two can be present together. One of the conditions for crown amputation is the absence of caudal stomatitis.[^wsava-dental-2020|Crown amputation]
- "It used to be called FORL, so it happens only in the root." The current name is tooth resorption. The stages include stages in which the crown has already been destroyed, and even a stage in which only remnants are left.[^avdc-nomenclature-2026|Stages of Tooth Resorption][^wsava-dental-2020|Classification]

## Questions to ask your vet

- What type and what stage this tooth is on the radiographs. The type decides whether the root is extracted whole, or whether the crown can be amputated when the conditions are met.[^wsava-dental-2020|Classification][^wsava-dental-2020|Crown amputation][^avdc-nomenclature-2026|Types of Resorption Based on Radiographic Appearance]
- Whether full-mouth dental radiographs were done. Radiographing only the mandibular third premolar is the sentinel approach when cost is limited. It is not a complete assessment.[^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings]
- If crown amputation is recommended, whether all five conditions are met, including the absence of caudal stomatitis. If one is missing, it is not an indication for this procedure.[^wsava-dental-2020|Crown amputation]
- How anesthetic risk is assessed. Advanced age, or another disease, is not a reason to withhold dental anesthesia in every such cat, but the plan for each cat is considered individually.[^aaha-dental-2019|page 10]
- How to judge, after the cat is home, whether pain is still present. An absence of pawing at the mouth does not mean that exposed dentin has stopped hurting.[^wsava-dental-2020|Clinical findings]
