## In one sentence

Hyperthyroidism is the most common endocrine disease of cats from middle age onward. Diagnosis rates in older cats around the world are about 1.5% to 11.4%; in the United States, they can be as high as 10% in cats 10 years and older.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Feline hyperthyroidism: an overview] Histologically, most cases are toxic nodular goiter, functioning autonomously and benign. Malignant carcinoma is about 2% at the first visit.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Feline hyperthyroidism: an overview] Most cats have disease on both sides together.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Feline hyperthyroidism: an overview] No single clinical finding can, by itself, prove that this is the disease. Because it is now often found earlier in the course, the signs can be very mild.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Diagnosis]

## Common signs

The typical picture is weight loss, increased food intake, increased urination, increased drinking, more vocalizing, restlessness, increased activity, rapid breathing, a rapid heart rate, vomiting, diarrhea, and an unkempt coat.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Presenting signs, differential diagnoses and diagnostic confirmation] The typical cat is older than 8 years, still lively and eating well, but losing weight. The litter box may need cleaning more often than before, which may be because the volume of urine has increased. Some cats go to drink from a faucet or from the water in a plant pot.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Signalment, history and physical examination] On physical examination, loss of muscle along the back and an unkempt coat are common. An enlarged thyroid on palpation points in this direction, but it cannot by itself prove that function is already increased. Heart murmurs and irregular heart rhythms are common. If the kidneys or the intestines feel wrong, a concurrent other disease has to be considered.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Signalment, history and physical examination]

Diseases that can look like this one also include diabetes mellitus, intestinal malabsorption, neoplasia (especially intestinal lymphosarcoma), chronic kidney disease, and parasites.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Presenting signs, differential diagnoses and diagnostic confirmation]

:::evidence-limited

The cause is not settled. Genetics may affect susceptibility: in one study, Siamese and Burmese cats were at lower risk. Indoor living, commercial food, and a longer lifespan have all been discussed. Chemicals listed by epidemiologic studies are associations, not causation that has already been proved. The guidelines state that no study has prospectively followed lifetime exposure, and every association an owner hears is still speculation.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Feline hyperthyroidism: an overview]

:::

## When to see a vet immediately

This disease is described as life-threatening and in need of prompt veterinary care. Untreated, it progresses and causes marked illness and death.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Treatment modalities][^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Prognosis]

- Labored breathing, collapse, or signs of congestive heart failure that are already present. Newly diagnosed hyperthyroidism that is not yet controlled, together with heart failure, means both diseases are treated at the same time.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Managing hyperthyroid cats with concurrent heart disease]
- Sudden loss of vision, or retinal detachment. Retinopathy caused by hypertension is looked for on examination of the fundus. Blood pressure and the retina are followed throughout treatment.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Signalment, history and physical examination]
- Eating almost nothing, or marked weakness. This can be another disease present at the same time, or it can be hypothyroidism or worsening kidney function after treatment. The goal of treatment is to restore normal thyroid function while avoiding hypothyroidism.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Treatment modalities][^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Managing the cat that is non-azotemic at initiation of treatment for hyperthyroidism]

Slow loss of weight while the amount eaten increases should lead to blood tests soon. There is no need to wait until the cat looks wasted. Screening of older cats puts total thyroxine into the panel so that the disease is found before the typical wasting, restlessness, and a swelling in the neck.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Abstract]

## Tests the clinic may use

Confirmation requires persistently elevated thyroid hormone (total T4, or total T4 plus free T4 by equilibrium dialysis), together with one or more typical signs.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Presenting signs, differential diagnoses and diagnostic confirmation] The minimum data when the disease is suspected are a blood count, serum biochemistry, a urinalysis, and total T4. Free T4, thyroid-stimulating hormone, a technetium-99m scan, or a T3 suppression test is sometimes also needed. Chest radiographs, echocardiography, and abdominal imaging are used to assess disease outside the thyroid.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Signalment, history and physical examination]

Blood pressure is measured at every visit. The clinic setting makes blood pressure hard to interpret, so a complete examination of the fundus is used to look for hypertensive retinopathy. If hypertension is still present after thyroid function has returned to normal, chronic kidney disease, diabetes mellitus, hyperaldosteronism, and hyperadrenocorticism are looked for again. Some cats develop hypertension only after thyroid function has returned to normal.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Signalment, history and physical examination]

Kidney function is distinguished before treatment starts, and IRIS staging is used, including blood pressure and urine protein. Loss of muscle raises blood urea nitrogen because protein turnover increases, and it makes creatinine low because there is less muscle. Body condition and muscle score are recorded at every examination.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Managing hyperthyroid cats with concurrent CKD]

## Treatment approaches

<!-- nonclaim --> This page does not give doses, dosing intervals, or course lengths. The amount is adjusted by the veterinarian who has examined this cat.

The goal is to restore normal thyroid function, avoid hypothyroidism, and reduce the adverse effects of treatment. The guidelines recommend treating every cat who is diagnosed, and treating concurrent disease at the same time. The choice depends on age, other diseases, cost, whether that treatment is available locally, and the veterinarian's experience. The four methods in common use are radioactive iodine, methimazole or carbimazole, surgical removal, and an iodine-restricted prescription diet. Percutaneous ethanol or thermal ablation is rarely used.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Treatment modalities] The overall success of management is 83% to 99%, depending on the disease and the method. Radioactive iodine and surgery have a chance of dealing permanently with adenoma and carcinoma. Drugs and diet control the clinical disease. They are used when the disease is milder, or when the cat has important other disease at the same time.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Summary Points]

Experts generally agree that, for most cats, radioactive iodine is the first choice. One treatment has a chance of eliminating benign functional thyroid tissue. It can also deal with functional tissue outside the thyroid, which may occur in 10% to 20%, it does not require general anesthesia, and adverse effects are few. Cats whose cardiovascular, kidney, gastrointestinal, or endocrine disease (for example diabetes mellitus) is already clearly unstable may not be good candidates, because isolation is required after treatment. Isolation time depends on local radiation rules and on the amount used, and it ranges from 3 days to 4 weeks.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Radioactive iodine] In most studies, the success rate of a single treatment exceeds 95%. T4 enters the reference range in 4 to 12 weeks. Complete resolution of clinical signs may take several months. The roughly 5% in whom it does not succeed usually have a larger tumor, more severe signs, a higher T4, or carcinoma. In cats who do not have carcinoma, a second treatment is usually still effective.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Radioactive iodine]

:::evidence-limited

Depending on the amount used, up to 75% of cats are hypothyroid for some period after treatment. Permanent hypothyroidism is not common, because radioactive iodine mainly destroys the overactive cells. A higher amount is more likely to injure normal cells. Up to 30% are still hypothyroid at three months, and about half of those have clinical signs or worsening kidney function and need thyroxine supplementation. These percentages come from studies that used different methods of choosing the amount. They are not a prediction for every cat. No method of calculation guarantees success.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Radioactive iodine]

:::

Methimazole and carbimazole can be used alone for the long term, or for a short period to stabilize the cat before surgery or radioactive iodine. Carbimazole is a metabolite of methimazole. Its actions and adverse effects are similar. It was not available in the United States at that time, and other countries use it. Methimazole blocks thyroid peroxidase and thereby inhibits synthesis of thyroid hormone. It does not destroy tissue that has already become hyperplastic or adenomatous, so with long-term use the nodules still grow, the amount of drug required may keep increasing, and in the end some cats cannot tolerate it or become completely refractory and have to be changed to another method.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Medical therapy] In most cats, T4 enters the normal range within 2 to 3 weeks of starting, so blood is drawn during that time. Clinical improvement may have to wait until T4 has stayed inside the reference range for 2 to 6 weeks. A transdermal preparation is useful for cats who will not cooperate with oral medicine.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Medical therapy]

Severe but uncommon adverse effects are liver injury and marked abnormalities of the blood cells (severe decrease in white cells, anemia, and thrombocytopenia). Gastrointestinal upset, lethargy, and facial itching vary in how often they occur. These adverse effects have not been shown to be related to the amount given. Most appear in the first 4 to 6 weeks and are less common after two to three months. Gastrointestinal upset may be somewhat less with the transdermal preparation.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Side effects of methimazole]

Surgery can be curative. The approaches include removal on both sides, removal on one side for truly unilateral disease, and staged removal on both sides. Anesthesia and surgery sometimes carry considerable complications and mortality. Reported hypocalcemia ranges from 6% to 82%, depending on the procedure. When the parathyroid glands are carefully preserved, hypocalcemia can be mild and brief. Severe hypocalcemia can be temporary or permanent. Other complications include Horner syndrome, laryngeal paralysis, and recurrence. About 4% to 9% of cats have ectopic adenomatous tissue, which is easy to leave behind at surgery. A preoperative isotope scan can reduce incomplete removal. In most studies, more than 90% reach normal thyroid function after surgery, and the recurrence rate within three years is close to 5%. Success depends heavily on stabilization before surgery and on the surgeon's experience.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Surgical thyroidectomy]

An iodine-restricted prescription diet uses the fact that making thyroid hormone requires iodine from the food. When the owner truly complies, 75% of cats have a clear fall in T4 and improvement in signs within 28 days. Cats whose T4 is very high may not become normal until day 180, and some never reach it. In a one-year study, 83% went into remission. Palatability problems affected 12% of the cats who were studied. In multi-cat homes, in cats who need another kind of prescription diet, when a medicine contains iodine, and in cats who go freely indoors and outdoors, this diet is hard to use or should not be used. The long-term consequences are unknown.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Dietary therapy][^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Expected outcome]

Whichever treatment is used, the first follow-up is 2 to 4 weeks after the start. After each change in the amount, another 2 to 4 weeks pass before the next check. Cats who are stable and do not have complicated other disease then have T4, a blood count, biochemistry, and a urinalysis measured every 4 to 6 months. When kidney function is impaired, T4 is kept in the upper half of the reference range.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Monitoring hyperthyroid patients]

When azotemia is present at the same time, both diseases are treated together. IRIS stage 1 and stage 2 can be monitored in the same way as a cat who is not azotemic. If kidney function is stable under reversible treatment, irreversible treatment is then considered. Stage 3 and stage 4 are managed more conservatively, for example with a lower amount of methimazole, and the kidney disease is treated more actively. Deliberately leaving "a little" thyrotoxicosis, in the hope that it will increase renal blood flow and make the creatinine come out low, is harmful. An elevated T4 increases cardiac output, volume load, sodium retention, renal hypertension, and glomerulosclerosis. In the end it causes chronic kidney disease to occur or to worsen, while giving a false impression that the creatinine still looks acceptable.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Managing the cat that is non-azotemic at initiation of treatment for hyperthyroidism][^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Managing the cat that is azotemic at initiation of treatment for hyperthyroidism] Cats who become azotemic only after treatment have a significantly shorter survival time. Iatrogenic hypothyroidism is to be avoided. Serial creatinine, T4, and thyroid-stimulating hormone are read together, and that is used to decide whether thyroxine supplementation is needed.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Managing the cat that is non-azotemic at initiation of treatment for hyperthyroidism]

Heart disease can be a direct effect of this disease, and it can also be unrelated. Once thyrotoxicosis and systemic hypertension have been dealt with, heart disease improves in some cats. In the months after success, abnormalities on echocardiography can newly appear, and they can also resolve. NT-proBNP is elevated both in this disease and in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, and it usually falls within three months of reaching normal thyroid function. If it is still high after three months, whether it is hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is assessed again.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Managing hyperthyroid cats with concurrent heart disease]

## Care at home

Record body weight, the amount eaten, drinking, vomiting, and whether activity has increased or the cat has become weak. Facial itching, marked lethargy, pale gums, or jaundice should be reported to the clinic, because adverse effects of the drug can appear in the first weeks.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Side effects of methimazole] When an iodine-restricted diet is used, this cat cannot also eat other food that contains iodine, treats, or flavored medicines that contain iodine, or the diet does not hold. In a multi-cat home, ask first how to feed so that the other cats are not eating the same food for a long time.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Expected outcome]

No method of prevention has been proved. The guidelines require that associations with the environment and with food be treated as speculation that has not been confirmed, not as something one home measure can guarantee will keep the disease from occurring.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Feline hyperthyroidism: an overview] Screening of older cats puts total T4 into the panel in order to find the disease earlier in its course. The summary states that about 10% of cats 10 years and older in the United States develop this disease. That is finding it early. It is not prevention.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Summary Points]

## Outlook and long-term care

Earlier studies reported survival of about 2 years after diagnosis. In newer data, cats without concurrent chronic kidney disease can have a median survival of up to 5.3 years. In cats who are well managed, illness and death are influenced more by disease that is present at the same time than by the thyroid disease itself.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Prognosis] The prognosis of thyroid carcinoma is somewhat worse than that of hyperplasia or adenoma. After appropriate treatment, these cats still often die of disease that is unrelated to the thyroid.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Prognosis] After radioactive iodine, return for the visits that were agreed, because hypothyroidism and azotemia can appear only after treatment has succeeded.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Radioactive iodine][^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Monitoring hyperthyroid patients]

## Common misunderstandings

- "If no lump can be felt in the neck, this disease is not present." An enlarged thyroid on palpation is only a pointer. Confirmation looks at persistently elevated thyroid hormone plus typical signs. Early on there may be no obvious swelling.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Signalment, history and physical examination][^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Presenting signs, differential diagnoses and diagnostic confirmation]
- "A large appetite means the cat is healthy." Weight loss together with an increased appetite is the typical combination in this disease. A good appetite is not a reason to skip testing.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Presenting signs, differential diagnoses and diagnostic confirmation]
- "Leaving the thyroid a little high keeps the kidneys safer." The guidelines state that doing this is harmful, and that the creatinine is made falsely low. What is to be avoided is hypothyroidism, not the maintenance of thyrotoxicosis.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Managing the cat that is non-azotemic at initiation of treatment for hyperthyroidism]
- "After the medicine has been taken for a while, the thyroid nodules disappear." Methimazole does not destroy hyperplastic or adenomatous tissue. The nodules can keep growing, the amount of drug may have to increase, and some cats eventually need a different treatment.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Medical therapy]
- "Switching to an ordinary low-iodine food is enough." What the guidelines discuss is a specific iodine-restricted prescription diet. Multi-cat homes, cats who need another prescription diet, and cats who will eat other things outside are often not suitable. The long-term consequences are unknown.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Dietary therapy][^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Expected outcome]
- "The cause has been found, and avoiding one kind of canned food or one kind of litter means the cat will not get it." What has been published is association. There is no prospective study of lifetime exposure. The guidelines call these associations speculation.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Feline hyperthyroidism: an overview]

## Questions to ask your vet

- Whether the total T4 was high once, or is persistently high, and whether typical signs are present at the same time. Confirmation needs both together.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Presenting signs, differential diagnoses and diagnostic confirmation]
- What the IRIS stage, blood pressure, and urine protein were before treatment. Low muscle makes creatinine look lower.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Managing hyperthyroid cats with concurrent CKD]
- Whether this cat is suited to radioactive iodine, medicine, surgery, or an iodine-restricted diet. A cat with unstable heart disease, kidney disease, or diabetes may not be suited to radioactive iodine, which requires isolation.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Treatment modalities][^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Radioactive iodine]
- If medicine is used, when to return for a T4 and for a look at adverse effects. In most cats T4 changes within 2 to 3 weeks, and severe adverse effects are mostly in the first 4 to 6 weeks.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Medical therapy][^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Side effects of methimazole]
- If creatinine rises after treatment, whether thyroxine should be supplemented, or whether the cat should also be managed as having chronic kidney disease. Deliberately maintaining thyrotoxicosis is not a way to protect the kidneys.[^aafp-hyperthyroid-2016|Managing the cat that is non-azotemic at initiation of treatment for hyperthyroidism]
