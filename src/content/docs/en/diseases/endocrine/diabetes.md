## In one sentence

Diabetes mellitus is blood glucose that stays high over the long term. In cats, the cause is a fall in the ability of pancreatic islet beta cells to secrete insulin, or a poorer response of the tissues to insulin, or both.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Introduction] In most affected cats the situation is close to type 2 in humans: beta-cell dysfunction plus insulin resistance. Immune-mediated type 1 is rare in cats. The beta-cell problem is related to islet amyloid deposition, glucose toxicity, and possibly oxidative stress and cytokines. A common cause of resistance is obesity. It can also come from other endocrine diseases, drugs (glucocorticoids, progestogens), and pancreatitis.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Epidemiology, pathogenesis and diagnosis] Prevalence in first-opinion practice is estimated at about 1 in 100 to 1 in 500, and it may still be increasing, partly in relation to obesity becoming more common.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Introduction]

## Common signs

The typical signs are increased urination, increased drinking, lethargy, weight loss, and increased food intake. Less common are weakness, a plantigrade stance with the paw on the ground, depression, and not eating. Not eating is seen especially in ketoacidosis.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Clinical signs and risk factors] The signs come from persistent high blood glucose and glucose in the urine. If it is not treated, or if control is poor, ketoacidosis appears in the end.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Introduction] Cats with long-term high blood glucose can develop peripheral neuropathy.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Diagnosis and Assessment]

Risk factors that have been reported include obesity (up to four times higher than in cats at an ideal weight), age 7 years and older, Burmese cats in studies from Australia, New Zealand, and Europe, indoor cats with little activity, male cats and neutered cats, and glucocorticoids and progestogens.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Clinical signs and risk factors] Concurrent disease that was present but not found (for example acromegaly) may have affected the results of these studies.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Clinical signs and risk factors]

## When to see a vet immediately

- Depression, not eating, vomiting, weakness, collapse, or a moribund state. Ketoacidosis is suspected. Confirmation needs these findings, plus high ketones in blood or urine, metabolic acidosis, and persistent high blood glucose. Ketoacidosis means hospitalization, with intensive treatment and monitoring.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Evaluation of the diabetic cat][^isfm-diabetes-2015|Diabetic ketoacidosis] A diabetic cat who is clinically ill and has ketones should be hospitalized; if the clinic cannot care for the cat all day, the cat is transferred to an emergency or specialty service.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Treatment]
- Seizures, lying flat, not eating, trembling, vomiting, unsteady walking, or unusual quietness. These are findings of hypoglycemia, and they can be life-threatening. The first aid at home that the guidelines give owners is to rub honey or glucose thoroughly onto the mucous membranes, and they advise keeping glucose gel in the house. Severe hypoglycemia still needs the clinic.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Hypoglycaemia]
- A cat on an SGLT2 inhibitor whose appetite worsens, who vomits, or who is markedly lethargic. The monitoring table in the review writes these situations as: stop that drug, change to insulin, and check ketones. Ketoacidosis is still possible when the blood glucose does not look very high.[^cook-sglt2-2024|Monitoring][^niessen-velag-2024|3.7. Ketonuria and suspected DKA]

Drinking more, urinating more, and weight loss should lead to blood tests and a urine test soon. There is no need to wait until the cat stops eating. Early high blood glucose, before there are signs, can be seen on a routine blood test, when the cat's weight is stable and the cat looks healthy. That is not yet clinical diabetes.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Diagnosis and Assessment]

## Tests the clinic may use

The diagnosis is usually persistent high blood glucose plus glucose in the urine, together with matching clinical signs. High blood glucose and glucose in the urine caused by stress are excluded first. Stress rarely pushes blood glucose above 16 mmol/L (288 mg/dL), although it can occasionally be very high, and it generally comes down within a few hours. Repeat blood and urine tests, or monitoring at home, are used to confirm that it persists.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Diagnosis] Fructosamine reflects the average blood glucose of about the previous week. Short-term stress hyperglycemia usually does not affect it. It helps confirm the diagnosis and later monitoring, but it may not yet be high when the disease has just started or is very mild.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Diagnosis]

Evaluation includes the history and physical examination, serum biochemistry, and a complete urinalysis. Bacterial culture is preferable when the sediment is active. Ideally there is also a blood count and fructosamine. Thyroid hormone is measured in older cats, to exclude hyperthyroidism. In a depressed cat, or a cat who is not eating, pancreatic ultrasound or pancreatic lipase is considered, because pancreatitis is common, but interpretation of these tests is not straightforward.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Evaluation of the diabetic cat] Diseases that will get in the way of the response to treatment are also looked for, for example kidney disease and hyperadrenocorticism, along with obesity and drugs that antagonize insulin.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Diagnosis and Assessment]

Not every cat with ketones in the blood or ketones in the urine has ketoacidosis. Cats with ketoacidosis have a low blood pH and look ill. A cat who has ketones but does not have acidosis can be managed as a cat without ketones.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Diabetic ketoacidosis]

## Treatment approaches

<!-- nonclaim --> This page does not give doses, dosing intervals, or course lengths. The type of insulin and the amount can be decided only by a veterinarian.

The mainstay of treatment for clinical diabetes is insulin plus adjustment of the diet. The goal is, as far as possible, to keep blood glucose through the day below the renal threshold, so that signs improve, while avoiding hypoglycemia that is clinically meaningful.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Treatment] The 2015 consensus writes the renal threshold as about 14 mmol/L (252 mg/dL) in most cats. Keeping blood glucose below this threshold can avoid osmotic diuresis and may reduce glucose toxicity.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Controlling hyperglycaemia] The harm of hypoglycemia, and the owner's worry about it, is large, so the view of the consensus is: if the two cannot both be achieved, it is preferable to allow a period of high blood glucose rather than to cause hypoglycemia.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Overall goals in managing diabetic cats] Hypoglycemia is defined as blood glucose below 3.0 to 3.5 mmol/L (54 to 63 mg/dL). When it is mild the cat may tolerate it and the owner may not notice; when it is severe it is life-threatening, and reactive hyperglycemia may follow.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Avoiding hypoglycaemia]

Insulins suitable for long-term use fall into several classes. Glargine, detemir, and protamine zinc insulin last longer in cats than lente, so they are more likely to control the cat better at the frequency the veterinarian selects. Different studies do not agree completely on the duration of action of the same insulin, and one cat differs from another.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Insulin choices][^isfm-diabetes-2015|Recommendations for insulin preparation and frequency of dosing] The 2018 AAHA guidelines likewise treat insulin as the mainstay, and list glargine and protamine zinc insulin as the longer-acting choices commonly used in cats.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Treatment] The unit concentration of insulin is not the same for every product. Some are 100 units per milliliter, and some are 40 units. The syringe or pen must match the concentration of that vial.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Recommendations for insulin preparation and frequency of dosing]

A low-carbohydrate diet may improve control. Two randomized controlled studies reported, respectively, a higher remission rate and better control of blood glucose, but a diet cannot change only the carbohydrate variable. The limit most of the panel recommend is 12% or less of metabolizable energy, or 3 grams per 100 kcal, while waiting for more research. Most wet foods, and therapeutic dry foods designed for diabetes, are lower in carbohydrate. Most ordinary dry foods are not the first choice. If another disease requires a different food, insulin alone can still provide control.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Choosing an optimum diet for cats with DM] AAHA likewise recommends about 12% of metabolizable energy, and states that experts do not agree on this number. High-fiber diets are usually not recommended for diabetes in cats.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Treatment] At the start, the pathologic loss of weight is stopped first, so food can be available at any time or given as several meals through the day. Obesity causes resistance. After blood glucose is moderately stable, planned weight loss may improve control and increase the chance of remission. During weight loss, blood glucose and the need for insulin are watched closely.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Practical weight loss in the overweight or obese cat with DM]

There is no good evidence to support oral drugs that lower blood glucose as better than insulin.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Role of oral hypoglycaemic agents] The 2015 consensus considered that, at that time, only glipizide had enough evidence to be used as the sole treatment when an owner initially refuses insulin. Its adverse effects include cholestasis, hypoglycemia, and vomiting, and this whole class of drugs that promote insulin secretion carries a concern that islet amyloidosis will progress. If a clinical response is going to appear, it is usually in 4 to 6 weeks. Many owners change to insulin after they find that it is ineffective. The earlier the change, the less likely they are to miss the window in which glucose toxicity can still be reversed.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Table 1][^isfm-diabetes-2015|Recommendations on using oral hypoglycaemics] The 2018 AAHA position is narrower: oral drugs that lower blood glucose are not recommended and are not regarded as suitable for long-term use. They are used only for a short time, and they must be combined with adjustment of the diet, when the owner refuses insulin or is considering euthanasia.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Treatment]

Remission is a reasonable goal in cats. It means insulin from outside the body is no longer needed. Remission can be only temporary.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Epidemiology, pathogenesis and diagnosis][^aaha-diabetes-2018|Introduction] When urine glucose stays negative, blood glucose measurements are all in the normal range (about below 7.5 mmol/L, 135 mg/dL), or fructosamine is below 350 µmol/L, the veterinarian usually reduces the insulin. Only after insulin has been stopped, if negative urine glucose or normal blood glucose is still maintained, is remission considered possible. Cats in remission still eat a low-carbohydrate diet, and signs are watched closely for a return.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Diabetic remission] Predictors of remission listed by AAHA include reaching very good control of blood glucose within six months of diagnosis, intensive monitoring at home, stopping drugs that antagonize insulin, and using glargine or detemir together with a low-carbohydrate diet.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Treatment]

:::evidence-limited

Reported remission rates range from 15% to 100%. A range this wide cannot tell an owner whether one particular cat will go into remission. Better control of blood glucose appears to be associated with a higher chance of remission, but that is not a guarantee.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Introduction][^isfm-diabetes-2015|Controlling hyperglycaemia]

:::

SGLT2 inhibitors became a treatment option for cats only after those two guidelines, from 2015 and 2018. The two currently approved in the United States for use alone are bexagliflozin and velagliflozin, and both are oral.[^cook-sglt2-2024|SGLT2 INHIBITORS AS SOLE THERAPY FOR FELINE DIABETES] The label and this review write the ideal cat for use as otherwise healthy, newly diagnosed, eating well, and without important comorbidity. Screening looks at whether ketosis is present, at kidney function, at the exocrine pancreas, and at other endocrine disease.[^cook-sglt2-2024|Patient selection and screening] They lower blood glucose by having the renal tubules take back less glucose. The premise is that the cat still has a certain amount of the cat's own insulin. When endogenous insulin is not enough, metabolism can shift into ketoacidosis, and the blood glucose at that time can be not high.[^niessen-velag-2024|4. DISCUSSION]

One field study of bexagliflozin enrolled 84 newly diagnosed cats. Of the 81 that could be evaluated on day 56, 68 (84%) reached that study's definition of treatment success. Vomiting occurred in 42 cats (50%), diarrhea in 32/84 (38%), decreased appetite in 31/84 (37%), and lethargy in 17/84 (20%). Vomiting and a fall in appetite can also come from ketoacidosis and are not necessarily a direct effect of the drug.[^cook-sglt2-2024|SGLT2 INHIBITORS AS SOLE THERAPY FOR FELINE DIABETES] The United States approval study of velagliflozin included 252 cats without ketones in the urine. Clinical hypoglycemia was not recorded within 180 days. The most common adverse event was a change in the character of the feces, in a little over half of the cats. Eighteen cats (7.1%) were diagnosed with ketoacidosis, 13 of them within 7 days of starting. Among the 214 cats who had never used insulin, 11 (5.1%) were affected; in cats who had previously used insulin, the proportion was close to 20%.[^cook-sglt2-2024|SGLT2 INHIBITORS AS SOLE THERAPY FOR FELINE DIABETES]

In a randomized, open trial compared with insulin, on day 45 the velagliflozin group reached that study's definition of success in 29/54 (54%) and the insulin group in 26/62 (42%), which met non-inferiority. The most common adverse event in the velagliflozin group was soft stool or diarrhea (23/61, 38%). Suspected ketoacidosis was 4/61 (7%). All four were found at unscheduled visits because the cat was unwell, and the blood glucose was not high at the time; the insulin group was 0/66. Of these 4 cats, 3 had not used insulin and 1 had. Hypoglycemia in the velagliflozin group was without clinical signs in every case.[^niessen-velag-2024|Abstract][^niessen-velag-2024|3.7. Ketonuria and suspected DKA][^niessen-velag-2024|3.6. Hypoglycemia]

:::evidence-limited

These trials excluded cats who already had ketones in the urine at screening, and not every newly diagnosed cat is suitable. Cats who had previously used insulin had more ketoacidosis in the larger approval series. When a small group already receiving insulin was changed to bexagliflozin, among the cats who stayed on the drug some developed a rise in blood beta-hydroxybutyrate, and three cats developed ketoacidosis. The review states that none of the existing reports has assessed the remission rate under an SGLT2 inhibitor. A fall in blood glucose cannot be described as the disease already being in remission.[^cook-sglt2-2024|SGLT2 INHIBITORS AS SOLE THERAPY FOR FELINE DIABETES][^cook-sglt2-2024|Diabetic remission]

:::

When control is unstable, check how the owner stores and injects the insulin, the food, the other drugs, and acromegaly, the thyroid, and the adrenal glands.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|The unstable diabetic cat]

## Care at home

Record drinking, urine volume, the amount eaten, body weight, and demeanor. Weight is checked at home or at the clinic every 1 to 2 weeks. Pathologic weight loss is stopped first. Planned weight loss starts only after the cat is already overweight and blood glucose has basic control.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Practical weight loss in the overweight or obese cat with DM] On a low-carbohydrate diet, meal times do not have to line up with the injection, because in studies of healthy cats a clinically important rise in blood glucose after a meal is not common. In practice, some veterinarians still have the cat eat at the time of the injection. At least two meals a day, kept to a fixed routine. For an obese cat the daily portion is measured. Unlimited supply is not suitable.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Recommended feeding regimen for cats with DM]

Measuring blood glucose at home can help with adjustment, but AAHA states that this is an ideal, not a requirement that every household can meet. The owner has to be able to recognize the signs of poor control.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Abstract] When an SGLT2 inhibitor is used, a blood glucose curve is usually not the focus of monitoring, because clinical hypoglycemia is uncommon. What is watched more closely is ketones, especially when treatment has just started, along with appetite, vomiting, and demeanor.[^cook-sglt2-2024|Monitoring] If the syringe is the wrong concentration, the insulin delivered will differ by a large amount. After the vial is opened, store it as the product instructions say. If the appearance changes color, or if it becomes abnormally cloudy, do not keep using it.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Recommendations for insulin preparation and frequency of dosing]

Obesity is a risk factor that can be changed. Weight loss improves resistance in a cat who already has diabetes, and obesity is also listed as a risk of developing the disease. Being indoors, with little activity, also increases the risk.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Clinical signs and risk factors][^isfm-diabetes-2015|Practical weight loss in the overweight or obese cat with DM] There is no evidence to support using a particular supplement in place of insulin or diet. Chromium and vanadium could matter only for a cat who is truly deficient, and the studies in diabetic cats are small.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Table 1]

## Outlook and long-term care

Cats who are well managed can have a reasonably good prognosis. Median survival in the studies is 13 to 29 months, longer in cats whose control is more stable, and many cats later die of disease other than diabetes.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Introduction] Euthanasia is sometimes because the owner's expectations were not met, or because of the burden of day-to-day care. The goals are therefore both to control the disease and to find a method the owner can keep doing.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Overall goals in managing diabetic cats] After remission, watching continues, because remission can be only temporary and the clinical signs can appear again.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Epidemiology, pathogenesis and diagnosis][^isfm-diabetes-2015|Diabetic remission] Reports on SGLT2 inhibitors do not answer the remission rate. Blood glucose that is under control is not the same as being free to stop the drug oneself.[^cook-sglt2-2024|Diabetic remission]

## Common misunderstandings

- "Drinking a lot of water means diabetes." Increased drinking and urination are also seen in chronic kidney disease and in hyperthyroidism. The diagnosis needs persistent high blood glucose and glucose in the urine, and stress hyperglycemia has to be excluded.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Diagnosis][^aaha-diabetes-2018|Diagnosis and Assessment]
- "An oral drug can replace insulin." In 2015 there was no evidence that oral drugs are better than insulin. The 2018 AAHA guidelines do not treat oral drugs as long-term treatment. SGLT2 inhibitors are a later, different class of oral drug. They suit only cats who have been screened, and they carry a risk of ketoacidosis.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Role of oral hypoglycaemic agents][^aaha-diabetes-2018|Treatment][^cook-sglt2-2024|Patient selection and screening]
- "A normal blood glucose means ketoacidosis cannot occur." In the velagliflozin trial, the cats in whom ketoacidosis was suspected did not have a high blood glucose at the visit.[^niessen-velag-2024|3.7. Ketonuria and suspected DKA]
- "Remission means cured, and the food can be changed back." Remission is defined as a period in which insulin is no longer needed. It may be temporary. Cats in remission should still eat a low-carbohydrate diet and be monitored.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Diabetic remission]
- "The cat will tell you about hypoglycemia." Mild hypoglycemia can be missed. Seizures, lying flat, trembling, or suddenly being unusually quiet need to be dealt with the same day, and sugar is rubbed on the mucous membranes as the veterinarian has instructed.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Avoiding hypoglycaemia][^isfm-diabetes-2015|Hypoglycaemia]

## Questions to ask your vet

- Whether this high blood glucose is stress, a drug, or diabetes that is persisting. Stress hyperglycemia generally comes down within a few hours.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Diagnosis]
- Whether ketones are present, and whether hospitalization is needed. A cat who is ill and has ketones should be hospitalized or referred.[^aaha-diabetes-2018|Treatment][^isfm-diabetes-2015|Diabetic ketoacidosis]
- Which concentration the insulin is, and whether the syringe or pen at home matches it. Concentrations that do not match deliver the wrong amount.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Recommendations for insulin preparation and frequency of dosing]
- If an SGLT2 inhibitor is being discussed, whether this cat is newly diagnosed, eating well, and free of ketones, and whether the kidneys and the pancreas have been assessed. Cats who had previously used insulin had more ketoacidosis in the approval series.[^cook-sglt2-2024|Patient selection and screening][^cook-sglt2-2024|SGLT2 INHIBITORS AS SOLE THERAPY FOR FELINE DIABETES]
- What counts as possible remission, and who decides to reduce or stop the drug. The owner does not stop insulin on their own because urine glucose has been negative for a few days.[^isfm-diabetes-2015|Diabetic remission]
