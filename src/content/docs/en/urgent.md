---
title: See a vet now
description: Situations that need a veterinarian or emergency clinic the same day. Do not wait overnight, and do not give human medicines.
---

If any situation below is happening, contact a veterinarian or emergency clinic now. Do not watch overnight, do not make the cat vomit, and do not give human painkillers, fever medicine, or leftover pet remedies. If breathing worsens on the way, or the cat becomes unconscious, tell the clinic you are coming.

This list is not every emergency. If something feels wrong, call.

## Little or no urine

A male cat that visits the litter box repeatedly, strains, cries, or passes only drops or no urine at all should be treated as a possible urethral obstruction until a veterinarian says otherwise. A bladder that stays full can cause pain, kidney injury, and a dangerous rise in potassium within hours. A female cat that suddenly passes no urine at all is an emergency too.

Blood in the urine, or frequent urination while urine is still coming, still needs a prompt visit. Complete obstruction should not wait. The [FLUTD](./diseases/urinary/flutd/) page is only a draft and does not replace this section.

## Hard breathing

Fast breathing at rest, heavy abdominal effort, open-mouth breathing, or blue or purple gums means respiratory distress. Keep the cat in a quiet, ventilated place. Do not press on the chest. Go now. Causes include fluid around the lungs, heart failure, severe anemia, air in the chest, and upper-airway obstruction. An owner cannot sort these out at home.

## Sudden hind-limb paralysis

Sudden weakness, cold hind legs, dragging, pain when the legs are touched, or pale pads can be an arterial blood clot. Cardiomyopathy in cats can throw a clot on an otherwise ordinary day. This is an emergency visit, not an orthopedic appointment for the next day.

## Seizure or sudden collapse

A seizure, a collapse, loss of awareness, or a cat that could walk and then cannot stand within minutes needs same-day assessment. Causes include toxins, severe high blood pressure, low blood sugar, neurologic disease, and other emergencies. During a seizure, move hard objects away. Do not put a hand in the mouth, and do not force food or water.

## Almost no food for about a day

Cats tolerate fasting poorly. About 24 hours with almost no food, especially with vomiting, fever, jaundice, existing weight loss, or in a kitten, senior, or overweight cat, should be seen the same day. Do not use human appetite stimulants or steroids to push through it.

## Possible poisoning

Lilies in the genera Lilium and Hemerocallis, including many florist lilies, are toxic to cat kidneys. Pollen, flowers, leaves, and vase water can all matter. Call a veterinarian even if the cat only may have licked a plant. Do not wait for vomiting or for urine to stop.

Permethrin in some dog flea spot-on products can cause tremors and seizures if it gets on a cat. Acetaminophen, ethylene glycol in some antifreeze, and anticoagulant rodenticides are emergencies too. If a product is on the coat, rinse with water while stopping the cat from licking it, and bring the package. Do not make the cat vomit unless a veterinarian tells you to.

## Collapse, abnormal gum color, or bleeding that will not stop

White, grey, or blue gums, cold legs, or a sudden collapse can mean shock, severe anemia, bleeding, or a heart problem. Nosebleeds, blood in urine, or a wound that keeps bleeding after pressure need emergency care.

## Repeated vomiting with a belly problem

Repeated vomiting with weakness, a swollen or painful belly, repeated unsuccessful attempts to pass stool, or possible swallowing of string, a rubber band, or a needle means no more food and no laxatives. Linear foreign bodies can cut into the intestine. Waiting makes that injury worse.
