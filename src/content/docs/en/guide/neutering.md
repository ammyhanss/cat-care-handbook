## What neutering is for

International Cat Care describes neutering as surgical removal of the reproductive organs, castration in males and spaying in females, and states that this is currently the only method that guarantees a cat will not reproduce. Controlling reproduction by neutering is described as vital in both owned and unowned cats. Pet cats can add to the number of cats without homes if they are not neutered before they can reproduce. iCatCare also links failure to neuter with behaviors associated with reproduction that often lead people to abandon cats, and then their kittens.[^icatcare-populations|Neutering]

The AAFP position supports pediatric spay and neuter, by 5 months of age, for cats who are not intended for breeding, as one part of preventing unplanned litters. Owners should be told that a cat can be reproductively active as early as 4 to 5 months, and that this can happen without obvious physical signs. The veterinarian judges the cat's health and their own surgical and anesthetic skill when deciding the age for that cat.[^aafp-neuter-2020|2020 Pediatric Sterilization in Cats]

The 2021 life stage guidelines list sterilization as a topic for the visits, and they point to the AAFP early-age position for the timing discussion. They do not reprint a single age in the visit table.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Discussion items for all life stages]

## The age is not one agreed month

:::evidence-limited

iCatCare tells owners to neuter pet cats at around four months of age, before the cat is capable of reproduction. The AAFP position says by 5 months, and it states that reproductive activity is possible at 4 to 5 months. Both are aiming at a time before an unplanned litter. They do not name the same month. Neither document, as used on this page, answers owner questions about joints, the urinary tract, or long-term behavior after early surgery. Those questions belong with the veterinarian who will operate. Do not treat a number from a website, including this one, as the booking date.[^icatcare-populations|Neutering of pet cats][^aafp-neuter-2020|2020 Pediatric Sterilization in Cats]

:::

In many places, neutering is difficult because of cost, transport, or a shortage of veterinarians, or because of religious or cultural beliefs. iCatCare lists the practical needs as being able to afford the service, being able to reach a clinic, and understanding why it is done.[^icatcare-populations|Neutering of pet cats]

## What still changes after surgery

Urine marking is most often seen in intact male cats, and the onset can coincide with sexual maturity. Both males and females may spray. One study reported that about 10% of sterilized cats still marked territory with urine. Neutering is still advisable. In a separate small study of 17 free-roaming cats, spraying almost disappeared after neutering. The life stage guidelines say neutering will not eliminate or prevent spraying in every cat, and that environmental stress can trigger marking, so the cat's environmental needs still have to be met.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Young adult cats][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Urine marking]

:::evidence-limited

The figure of about 10% comes from one study cited by the 2021 guidelines. The study of 17 cats is also a small series. Neither number is a prediction for one household.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Young adult cats][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Urine marking]

:::

Neutering is associated with weight gain, especially in males. The kitten visit is a time to talk about that risk. Free-choice feeding, with food always available, is a common owner habit and can lead a cat to eat more than it needs. Dietary energy restriction may be appropriate to prevent the gain. The amount is adjusted so the cat stays at a healthy body condition. Simply feeding less of an ordinary maintenance food, once a cat is already overweight, can leave the cat short of vitamins and minerals. A weight-loss diet is formulated to keep those nutrients up while the calories go down.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Kittens][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Young adult cats]

## What to arrange

- Book the surgery before the cat can reproduce. Ask the clinic which month they use for this cat, and why.[^aafp-neuter-2020|2020 Pediatric Sterilization in Cats][^icatcare-populations|Neutering of pet cats]
- Ask what to do about food before surgery. If fasting is needed, the clinic should give the timing so the cat is not fasted longer than the procedure requires. The cat-friendly environment guidelines point to a 3 to 4 hour fast after a small meal of wet food as sufficient in the anesthesia guidance they cite.[^isfm-aafp-clinic-2022|Preparation to reduce stress prior to the clinic visit]
