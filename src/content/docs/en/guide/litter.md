<!-- nonclaim --> This page is owner education about litter boxes. It does not diagnose house-soiling, and it does not give doses. A veterinarian has not reviewed it. If a cat strains to urinate and passes little or no urine, use [See a vet now](../urgent/) the same day.

## How cats use a toilet

The average cat eliminates about three to five times a day, so a small box fills up quickly. Most cats prefer a quiet place at the edge of their core area, away from food and rest. Many prefer separate sites for urine and feces. Covering waste is usual, and it often starts around 7 weeks of age, but some cats leave waste uncovered. That is not, by itself, a training failure.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box][^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Normal feline elimination behavior]

Kittens start to explore litter around 3 to 5 weeks and often eliminate in a box by 5 to 7 weeks. The life stage guidelines say elimination is usually away from the main resting place, and that some clinicians believe a kitten copies the queen's litter. Offering more than one substrate lets the kitten show a preference.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Normal feline elimination behavior][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Toileting]

## Number and place

A home with one cat should have two boxes, in two places. In a home with more than one cat, the 2014 guidelines call "one box per cat, plus one" a historical rule of thumb, not an absolute. Cats who are socially affiliated may share. Cats who are not affiliated should have separate stations. A multi-level home should have at least one box on each level. The guiding idea is enough boxes, in enough places, that each social group can toilet without passing a cat they avoid.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box]

The 2021 guidelines phrase the same rule as one box per cat plus one, or one box per social group plus one when the groups are known. They add that the spot should be quiet, convenient, and give the cat a way out.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|General litter box considerations]

Do not put food or water next to the box. Cats treat boxes that sit side by side as one big box. If two boxes must be near each other, the 2014 guidelines say their openings should be at right angles, or around a corner, so a cat can leave without meeting another cat head-on.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box][^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 2]

## Size, lid, and litter

Bigger is better. The 2014 guidelines recommend a rectangular box about 1.5 times the cat's length from nose to the base of the tail. In one study, adult cats preferred a box of 34 by 15 inches (86 by 39 cm) over a smaller one. The 2021 guidelines use 1.5 times the length from nose to the tip of the tail, and they say most manufactured boxes are too small. A storage container or a similar large tray is the workaround both documents describe. The side should be low enough for a kitten, a senior, or a cat with joint pain to step in.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|General litter box considerations]

:::evidence-limited

The two guidelines do not measure the cat at the same point. One stops at the base of the tail and the other includes the tail. Both state that ordinary manufactured boxes are often too small, and both want an entrance a kitten or a stiff cat can use. They do not settle which landmark to use.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|General litter box considerations]

:::

Research cited in 2014 found cats about equally split between large covered boxes and open boxes. Preference may depend more on cleaning than on the lid. Open boxes are recommended when possible, because the owner can see them and scoop them, and because a lid holds odor. A high side still gives some privacy and catches urine from a cat who stands. The opening has to stay easy.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box]

Litter is sold to people. Cats often find scented litter and deodorizers unpleasant. In most cases the 2014 guidelines suggest a fine, sand-like, unscented clumping litter, at least 1.25 inches (3 cm) deep. Avoid liners, slotted grills, and deodorizing powders. The 2021 guidelines say most adult cats prefer clumping litter and plain unscented litter, and that some cats find scented litter clearly aversive. For a kitten, start with unscented clumping litter, or the litter already in use, and offer a choice.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Young adult cats][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Table 2]

## Cleaning

Remove waste at least once a day and top up the litter. The 2014 guidelines say to wash the box every 1 to 4 weeks. Some behaviorists prefer weekly washing and a full litter change. Others find that every 2 to 4 weeks is accepted by the cat and by the owner. Use hot water. Avoid ammonia and strong-smelling chemicals.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box]

:::evidence-limited

The 2014 guidelines say to wash with soap and hot water. The 2021 guidelines say soap or strong chemicals should be avoided, and that hot water is best. They agree on scooping at least daily and on avoiding ammonia and strong chemicals. They do not agree about soap.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|General litter box considerations]

:::

The 2014 text also says that, unless waste is removed very often (they mention at least every 2 hours as the pace that would stop odor building up), many commercial boxes are simply too small. That sentence is an argument for a larger box and daily scooping. It is not a rule that a home must be scooped every 2 hours.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box]

## When the cat stops using the box

Do not punish toileting in the wrong place, and do not carry the cat to the box as a punishment. The environmental guidelines say the same about scent marking and about elimination outside the box. Punishment does not teach the location you want, and the life stage guidelines say fear rises when a cat's "stop" signals are ignored.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|General litter box considerations][^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 5][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Feline communication signs]

House-soiling has medical causes as well as box and social causes. A kitten who misses the box should be checked for a congenital urinary or gut problem, gut parasites, or another infection. An adult who misses the box may have a medical or a behavioral reason. Seek care promptly, both because urethral blockage can be life-threatening and because the habit becomes harder to change if it continues. Drinking more, or urinating large amounts, can come from diabetes, chronic kidney disease, or hyperthyroidism. Pain or stiff joints can make a high-sided box or a box up a flight of stairs unusable.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|General litter box considerations][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Senior cats]

## A workable setup

- One cat: two boxes, two rooms or two quiet corners.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box]
- Several cats: at least one more box than the number of cats, and more if the cats do not affiliate. The 2014 guidelines say that extra box is not an absolute when the cats are one social group.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|General litter box considerations]
- A box at least 1.5 times the cat's body length, with a low entrance for a kitten or a stiff cat.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|General litter box considerations]
- Unscented clumping litter, scooped at least daily, washed with hot water, and not with ammonia.[^aafp-isfm-litter-2014|Designing the optimal litter box]
- No food, no water, and no punishment at the box.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 2][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|General litter box considerations]
