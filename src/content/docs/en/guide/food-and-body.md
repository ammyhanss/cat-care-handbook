## A nutritional check at every visit

WSAVA asks clinics to make a nutritional assessment, and a specific feeding recommendation, for every patient at every visit. They call this the fifth vital assessment, after temperature, pulse, respiration, and pain. A short screen looks at age, body condition, muscle, whether the diet is unusual or homemade, medical conditions, and appetite. If any of those is a concern, a longer assessment follows.[^wsava-nutrition-2011|Nutritional evaluation]

The life stage guidelines ask about how much is eaten and drunk, how often, and the way the food is offered, and they record body weight, body condition score, and muscle condition score at every life stage. Photographs from above and from the side help the owner and the clinic see slow change.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Life stage nutrition and weight management][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Table 2]

## What "complete" means

Domestic cats have narrow nutritional requirements. All cats need protein. Of the 23 amino acids in protein, 11 are essential for the cat, and a missing essential nutrient can cause disease. The 2021 guidelines say that, at every life stage, cats should be fed a diet whose label carries an Association of American Feed Control Officials statement of nutritional adequacy. WSAVA also names FEDIAF, the European pet-food body, among the regional standards that set nutrient profiles. Use the statement that matches where the food is sold, and ask the clinic if the label is unclear.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|All life stages][^wsava-nutrition-2011|Nutritional evaluation]

Kittens can be weaned onto a commercially balanced kitten food from 3 to 5 weeks of age. Energy need falls quickly: the guidelines give about 200 kcal per kilogram of body weight per day at 10 weeks, and about 80 kcal per kilogram per day at 10 months. Those figures show why a kitten food and an adult food are not interchangeable. The amount for one cat is then adjusted to body condition, not copied from a single number.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Kittens]

Change a food over 7 to 10 days. For an older cat, the life stage guidelines suggest offering the new food in a second dish beside the familiar food, at home, rather than swapping the bowl in the clinic, where stress can create a food aversion.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|All life stages][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Mature adult and senior cats]

Cats often eat several small meals. Hiding a little food, or using a puzzle feeder, copies the search that is part of hunting, if the cat will use it. Each cat should be able to eat alone, in a quiet place, not beside another animal.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|All life stages][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Kittens]

## Water

Food and water are separate resources. Put them apart, and give the cat more than one place to drink. Toileting areas stay away from both.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 2]

The visit should include a question about how much the cat drinks. In mature adult and senior cats, drinking more and urinating more are history items that guide which tests are run. Increased drinking can be a sign of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, or hyperthyroidism, among other causes. It is not a diagnosis by itself.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Table 2][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Mature adult and senior cats][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Senior cats]

## Body condition and muscle

On the 9-point scale, the 2011 WSAVA guidelines set the goal for most pets at 4 or 5. They note that some owners will see that as too thin, so the explanation matters. Disease risk in adult animals appears to rise above a score of 6. The 2021 life stage guidelines call 6 or 7 out of 9 overweight, and 8 or above obese. Feeling the body is required, especially under a long coat. Ribs, waist, and abdominal fat are part of the score. Looking only is not enough.[^wsava-nutrition-2011|Condition scoring][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Young adult cats]

:::evidence-limited

The 2025 WSAVA cat chart adds that a score of 6 out of 9 may be acceptable in some cats, especially older cats. That note is not the same as the 2011 goal of 4 or 5, or the 2021 statement that 6 or 7 is overweight. An owner should not pick the higher number in order to skip a conversation about weight. The veterinarian scores the cat in the clinic.[^wsava-bcs-2025|Body Condition Score][^wsava-nutrition-2011|Condition scoring][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Young adult cats]

:::

Muscle condition is not the same as fat. WSAVA assesses muscle over the skull, shoulders, spine, and pelvis. An overweight cat can still be losing muscle. Muscle loss is easy to miss if only the fat over the ribs is checked. The 2011 guidelines say the simple muscle scale was still being validated, so a clinic uses it as a clinical observation, not as a finished research instrument.[^wsava-nutrition-2011|Condition scoring]

Being overweight is linked, in the studies the life stage guidelines cite, with diabetes, lameness, non-allergic skin disease, urethral obstruction, and, in one study, more oral disease. Published estimates of how common obesity is in cats range from 1.8% to 40%. Underweight is a common problem in senior cats, and digestive efficiency can fall, so some seniors need more calories, not fewer. Healthy mature and senior cats should not be put on a protein-restricted diet just because they are older. The guidelines describe a moderate protein level, about 30% to 45% of dry matter, for healthy cats in that age group. Cats who already have chronic kidney disease are a different case, and a renal diet is a veterinary decision.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Young adult cats][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Mature adult and senior cats]

Treats, snacks, and table food that add up to more than 10% of calories are one of the screening flags in the WSAVA table. An unconventional diet, including raw, homemade, or vegetarian food, is another.[^wsava-nutrition-2011|Table 1]

## Raw meat, and homemade food

AAHA and the AAFP do not advocate raw or dehydrated non-sterilized foods of animal origin, including treats. The WSAVA Global Nutrition Committee recommends that raw meat-based diets not be fed to dogs or cats. It states that there is currently no properly documented evidence of a health benefit, and that the risks are documented: bacterial and parasite contamination, nutritional imbalance, and, when bones are included, constipation, diarrhea, tooth fractures, and obstruction. Thyroid tissue in some raw diets has been linked to diet-induced hyperthyroidism.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|All life stages][^wsava-raw-2020|WSAVA Global Nutrition Committee Statement on Risks of Raw Meat-Based Diets]

WSAVA's nutrition guidelines add that a pet can shed pathogens in feces for a long time after eating contaminated raw meat, even when the pet looks well, and that a hospitalized pet on a raw diet is a risk to staff and other patients. Homemade food, cooked or raw, needs a check of the recipe by a board-certified veterinary nutritionist or an equivalent. Vegetarian foods are a particular risk for cats.[^wsava-nutrition-2011|Extended evaluation]

:::evidence-limited

These statements are committee recommendations. They do not report a head-to-head trial of every commercial raw product. They also do not say that a cooked homemade diet is automatically balanced. If an owner is considering either, the person to ask is a veterinarian, and for a home recipe, a veterinary nutritionist.[^wsava-raw-2020|WSAVA Global Nutrition Committee Statement on Risks of Raw Meat-Based Diets][^wsava-nutrition-2011|Extended evaluation]

:::

## At home

- Feed a food whose label states it is complete for the cat's life stage, under the standard used where the food is sold.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|All life stages][^wsava-nutrition-2011|Nutritional evaluation]
- Keep water available, away from the food and away from the litter box, in more than one place.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 2]
- Weigh the cat, or ask the clinic to, and learn what a score of 4 or 5 out of 9 feels like on the ribs and the waist.[^wsava-nutrition-2011|Condition scoring]
- Do not leave food out all the time if the cat is gaining weight. Free-choice feeding can lead to eating more than the cat needs.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Young adult cats]
- Call the clinic if drinking, appetite, or weight changes, especially in a cat over 7 years old.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Mature adult and senior cats]
