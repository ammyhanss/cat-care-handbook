## Let the cat choose the pace

Cats hunt alone, and escape matters more to them than a fight. A safe place lets a cat leave a situation that feels unfamiliar. Strange smells, loud or odd noises, unfamiliar objects, and unknown animals are the signals the guidelines name. Having the option to withdraw gives the cat some control.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 1]

When a cat is forced out of a familiar territory, or a new cat enters it, the usual response is avoidance or hiding. Fighting is described as a last resort, when escape is not possible.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Understanding the cat's needs and behavior]

Do not force contact. The person gets down to the cat's level, avoids a fixed stare, and lets the cat approach and sniff a hand. If the cat looks relaxed and wants contact, stroking the head and cheeks is the contact the guidelines call most appropriate. Gentle talk can help. When the cat walks away, the interaction is over.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 4]

Signs that a cat is willing to interact include a slow blink, a purr or chirp, rubbing the face or head on a person, trying to get onto a lap, staying close, or pushing the body into a hand. A relaxed roll that shows the belly is not an invitation to touch the belly. Many cats treat that area as vulnerable.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 4]

Research cited in the 2022 interaction guidelines found that contact lasts longer when the cat approaches the person than when the person approaches the cat.[^aafp-isfm-interact-2022|The cat as a species]

## Early weeks, and why the dates are not identical

:::evidence-limited

The documents do not use one window for early social experience. The 2013 environmental guidelines put the formative period for human handling at 2 to 7 weeks of age, and they say a negative experience in that period can leave a long-lasting fear. The 2021 life stage guidelines say the sensitive period for new experiences, people, and other animals begins as early as 2 to 3 weeks and may be closing by 9 to 10 weeks, that the window varies by cat, and that the quality of the experience is what matters. The 2022 interaction guidelines say experiences between 2 and 9 weeks can affect behavior for life, and that a kitten handled positively and often, by a range of people including children, is more likely to accept handling and to be less afraid of unfamiliar people. What the three documents share is that gentle, positive handling early on matters, and that a frightening first experience can last. They do not share one end date.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 4][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Behavior and environmental needs][^aafp-isfm-interact-2022|The individual cat]

:::

The 2021 guidelines say kittens should have pleasant interactions with people for 30 to 60 minutes a day, and that the kitten should be allowed to approach people and other pets. Pair new experiences with food or another reward. Avoid a first encounter that is stressful.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Behavior and environmental needs]

The 2013 guidelines add two figures from the studies they cite: ideally at least four people handle the kitten in that early period, and short sessions that add up to an hour a day were associated with friendly adult cats. Treat those figures as the older guideline's summary of the studies, not as a quota a new owner has failed if the numbers are different.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 4]

A kitten that is startled or handled roughly may keep that fear. Gentle handling is preparation for later care, including grooming, nail trims, and giving medicine. Almost every cat will need medicine at some point, so practicing with a soft treat, before a pill is required, is the method the life stage guidelines describe.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Training kittens in preparation to be adult cats]

## Play without using hands

Kittens learn predatory play by watching, swatting, chasing, pouncing, and catching. Social play between cats peaks around 12 weeks, and object play becomes more common after that. Throughout the first year, predatory-type play is still common. Do not use hands or feet as toys. The cat learns that hands are the toy, and people get scratched or bitten.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Behavior and environmental needs]

Move a wand so the toy travels like prey, either through the air or away along the ground, and let the cat catch it. Put string toys and toys with small parts away when play ends. Rotate toys so they stay interesting. Kittens need toys and puzzle feeders that fit their size. Older cats still need play, at a pace that fits them. In a home with more than one cat, play with each cat separately, and keep toys in more than one place.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 3]

The 2022 interaction guidelines make the same point about hands: play aimed at the hands can lead the cat to play with people's hands later, including during a veterinary exam. Object play is the alternative they name.[^aafp-isfm-interact-2022|The emotional state]

## Other cats

Cats do not welcome an unfamiliar cat into their territory, and they usually react with aggression. If the newcomer stays and becomes familiar, the cats may join one group over time. The guidelines call for a gradual increase in familiarity. If the cats who already live there do not feel secure, a new arrival brings stress and conflict. It is generally easier for adult cats to accept a kitten than another adult. Adopting cats who are already bonded, such as siblings, is preferable to adopting cats from different social groups.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Understanding the cat's needs and behavior]

Affiliative cats groom each other, touch noses, or sleep in contact. People often think cats get along because they gather at a food bowl. The life stage guidelines say that gathering can happen simply because the resource is in one place, and that the real signs of a friendly relationship are allogrooming, nose touching, or sleeping in contact.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Behavior and environmental needs]

Give every cat its own feeding station. Cats who are one social group still need resources separated from other groups. Hiding places, perches, toileting, food, and water in more than one location reduce fear.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 2][^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Understanding the cat's needs and behavior]

A cat who comes home smelling of a clinic can disrupt the shared scent of the household. Medicines, antiseptics, cleaners, and anesthetic gases are smells other cats can detect, and cats who had been friendly may then show aggression. The guidelines suggest arranging routine visits for all the cats at once when that is possible. When only one cat has been away, keep that cat in a separate room until the cats are calm, then reintroduce them with as little human directing as possible. If a fight starts, interrupt it without taking one cat's side.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 5][^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Tips for applying the Guidelines]

## Other people, and a dog

Introduce a kitten to people, including children, and to other animals gradually, and let the kitten approach. The life stage guidelines list children, noises, animals, car travel, and the veterinary clinic among the things to pair with food.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Behavior and environmental needs]

The same guidelines say kittens learn a fear response to other species, including people and dogs, from the queen, along with food acceptance and toileting habits. A first meeting that the kitten can leave is the pattern they describe. They do not describe a timed protocol for introducing a dog.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Behavior and environmental needs]

## What to watch for in the first weeks

- The cat has a place to hide, and contact stops when the cat leaves.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 1][^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 4]
- Play uses toys, not hands or feet.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Behavior and environmental needs]
- A new cat meets resident cats gradually, with separate resources, rather than being put straight into the shared rooms.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Understanding the cat's needs and behavior]
- After a clinic visit, a cat who lives with other cats gets a calm room before a full reintroduction.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Tips for applying the Guidelines]
- A change in how the cat eats, drinks, urinates, or uses the hiding places is a reason to call the clinic. The life stage guidelines treat changes in behavior as possible signs of illness or pain, not as "just the new home" once the cat has had time to settle, and they say senior cats with new behavior need a medical check. A newly arrived kitten who misses the litter box should also be checked for a medical cause, including parasites.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Discussion items for all life stages][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|General litter box considerations]
