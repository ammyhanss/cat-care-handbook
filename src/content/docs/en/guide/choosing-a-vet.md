## Why the visit itself matters

A clinic is outside the cat's territory. That alone can produce fear. Fear changes the examination: it can mislead the findings, distress the cat, slow recovery, and make the next visit harder. It also raises the chance that someone is bitten or scratched. The environment guidelines say a clinic does not need a renovation to improve this. Separating species completely is not a requirement for doing better.[^isfm-aafp-clinic-2022|Abstract][^isfm-aafp-clinic-2022|Clinical challenges]

Cats remember a single bad visit. The interaction guidelines say a negative experience becomes part of the next visit, even when nothing equally bad happens the second time. Avoiding the obvious mistakes, such as heavy restraint or scruffing, is not described as enough on its own. The cat also needs a sense of control.[^aafp-isfm-interact-2022|Introduction]

Surveys cited there found that most caregivers thought their cat's welfare was impaired at every stage of the visit, including before and after, and that travel, the waiting room, and the examination were the most stressful parts. About one-third of respondents in one survey said that seeing the cat's stress made them less willing to come back.[^aafp-isfm-interact-2022|Introduction]

## What to look for in the building

Ideally, cats wait where they cannot see, hear, or smell dogs. A quiet end of a hallway, with a chair, can be a cat waiting spot if a separate room does not exist. A separate entrance is better when the building allows it. The examination room should let the cat stay in the bottom of the carrier, on familiar bedding, rather than being tipped out onto a table.[^isfm-aafp-clinic-2022|Pre-consultation period and waiting area][^isfm-aafp-clinic-2022|Preparation to reduce stress prior to the clinic visit]

The carrier should live at home, open, in a place the cat already uses, so it is furniture rather than a signal of a trip. Cover it for the journey so sights and sounds are muffled, and watch airflow and heat. Support the carrier from the top and the bottom. Do not carry it by the handle or the strap alone. In a car, the guidelines cite the Center for Pet Safety recommendation that most carriers sit on the floor behind the front seats. If the cat is likely to be nauseated or very afraid in the car, ask the clinic about a prescribed medicine before the trip. This page does not name a dose.[^isfm-aafp-clinic-2022|Preparation to reduce stress prior to the clinic visit]

If a procedure requires fasting, the clinic should say how long. The environment guidelines warn against fasting longer than needed, and they cite a 3 to 4 hour fast after a small meal of wet food as sufficient in the anesthesia guidance they reference.[^isfm-aafp-clinic-2022|Preparation to reduce stress prior to the clinic visit]

## What to look for in the handling

Cat-friendly interaction means reading the cat's emotional state and giving the cat a perceived choice while the examination still gets done. Contact lasts longer when the cat approaches the person. Hiding, including in the carrier base or under a towel, is a normal way to cope. A treat, if it is offered to a frightened cat, should be placed on the surface, not pushed from a hand, so the cat can refuse it.[^aafp-isfm-interact-2022|The cat as a species][^aafp-isfm-interact-2022|The emotional state]

The life stage guidelines say to keep the mouth, the temperature, nail trims, blood samples, and imaging until the end, and to write down which parts stressed that cat so the next visit can use the same order. A towel that the cat already relaxes in, a favorite treat, and a record of what worked last time are the examples they give. Aversive handling and punishment should always be avoided. Positive reinforcement and food are the tools they name for helping a kitten accept handling.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Importance of feline-friendly handling][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Discussion items for all life stages][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Incorporating kitten socialization into the examination visit]

A first kitten visit is a chance to make the clinic familiar. The team should be able to show you the cat's fear signals, including cowering, flattened ears, and hissing, and should let the kitten explore.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Incorporating kitten socialization into the examination visit]

## Questions to ask before you book

- Where will the cat wait, and can that place be away from dogs?[^isfm-aafp-clinic-2022|Pre-consultation period and waiting area]
- Will the examination start in the carrier, and will someone write down what this cat tolerated?[^isfm-aafp-clinic-2022|Preparation to reduce stress prior to the clinic visit][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Importance of feline-friendly handling]
- How does the clinic handle a cat who is too afraid for a full examination that day? The interaction guidelines say restraint increases fear, and that a plan may include going home and trying again after preparation, rather than forcing the visit.[^aafp-isfm-interact-2022|Introduction][^aafp-isfm-interact-2022|Negative (protective) emotions]
- What should you practice at home with the carrier, and do they want you to call before the visit if the cat was distressed last time?[^isfm-aafp-clinic-2022|Preparation to reduce stress prior to the clinic visit]
