## Five needs, in ordinary rooms

The 2013 guidelines apply to every pet cat, whatever the lifestyle. The five pillars are a safe place; multiple, separated resources; play and predatory-type behavior; human contact that is positive, consistent, and predictable; and respect for smell.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Abstract][^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Five pillars of a healthy feline environment]

![Five numbered circles. 1 is an arch with an opening. 2 is a separated oval, drop shape, and rectangle. 3 is a stick and a circle. 4 is an arc and a circle with space between them. 5 is a dot and three curved lines.](/diagrams/five-pillars.svg)

**Figure.** Circles 1 to 5 are the five pillars in the 2013 guidelines, in the order given in the summary: a safe place; multiple and separated resources; play and predatory behavior; human-cat contact that is positive, consistent, and predictable; and respect for the cat's scent. The drawing has no words. The numbers are the order. [^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Five pillars of a healthy feline environment]

A safe place is often raised, private, and easy to enter. A box, a covered perch, or a carrier with the cat's bedding all count. There should be at least as many of these places as there are cats, spaced apart, and a kitten or an older cat needs them low enough to reach.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 1]

If every resource sits in one spot, cats in a shared home have to queue or avoid each other. Spreading food, water, toilets, scratching surfaces, and beds lets a cat use them without a confrontation. One cat still needs a choice: the example given is at least two resting places, two feeding places, and two toileting places.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 2]

## Scratching

Scratching is normal. It shortens and conditions the claw, and it leaves scent from glands in the feet. Posts should be available from the day the cat arrives, near resting places and along routes the cat uses. In one study, rope was used most often when it was offered. Cats aged 10 to 14 years preferred carpet. The posts used more often were simple uprights, or trees at least 3 feet high with two or more levels, and a base no wider than 3 feet. Keeping nails shorter can reduce damage to furniture and to people. Meeting the need to scratch can reduce scratching of the sofa. The life stage visit includes claw care and alternatives to declawing.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Training kittens in preparation to be adult cats][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Table 2][^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 5]

## Play and hiding

Predatory behavior continues in cats who are well fed. The sequence the guidelines describe is locate, stalk and chase, pounce, then handle and eat the prey. If a cat cannot do a version of that, the result they name is obesity, or boredom and frustration that can show up as overgrooming, stress-related disease, or aggression aimed at the wrong target.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 3]

Use a wand toy that moves like prey, and let the cat catch it. Puzzle feeders and scattered pieces of dry food make the cat search. Rotate toys. Put string and small parts away afterward. Do not play with hands or feet. Kittens need more intense play than adults, and older cats still need play at a pace they can manage. In a multi-cat home, play with each cat apart from the others.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 3][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Behavior and environmental needs]

Hiding is a coping behavior. A cat who can withdraw has some control, and control itself is described as satisfying. A new object, a loud noise, or a visitor is a reason to leave the hiding place available, not a reason to pull the cat out to be social.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 1]

Declining play in a young adult makes weight gain more likely. One study found that three sessions of 10 to 15 minutes of exercise a day, without cutting food, was associated with a loss of about 1% of body weight in a month. That is one study's result, not a promise for every cat.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Play]

## People

Many cats prefer frequent, low-intensity contact that they start, pause, and end. Get down, avoid a hard stare, and let the cat sniff a hand. Head and cheeks are the areas the guidelines call the most appropriate to stroke. Stop when the cat leaves. Individual cats differ in whether they want petting, grooming, play, talk, being picked up, or a lap. Learn the cat in front of you. A change in that preference, especially with age, can also be illness, so a sudden refusal to be touched is a reason to call the clinic.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 4]

Positive reinforcement, such as a treat for coming when called, is the training method the life stage guidelines describe. It is also how they suggest building a better association with the carrier, grooming, nail trims, and later medication. Aversive handling and punishment should always be avoided, including during a veterinary visit.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Training kittens in preparation to be adult cats][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Incorporating kitten socialization into the examination visit]

## Why punishment does not work

The life stage table tells clinics to discourage punishment and to encourage positive reinforcement when an owner asks about unwanted behavior. Scent marking and elimination outside the box should never be punished. A cat who toilets in the wrong place should not be scolded and should not be carried to the box as a penalty.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Table 2][^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 5][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|General litter box considerations]

Cats are stealthy about anxiety. A content cat holds the ears forward, keeps the whiskers loose, and wraps the tail loosely. Fear can look like a low head, a crouch, freezing, hiding, frantic flight, ears flat or turned, wide pupils, whiskers forward, or a tail that twitches. Vocal threat, such as a hiss, yowl, or growl, is part of the same picture. The guidelines say the cat must be able to signal "stop." When that signal is ignored, fear increases and the signal escalates.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Feline communication signs]

If the unwanted behavior is house-soiling, scratching the furniture, or conflict with another cat, the first checks are the resources and a medical exam, not a consequence. House-soiling and aggression toward people or other pets are among the common reasons cats are given up.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Behavior and environmental needs][^aaha-aafp-life-2021|General litter box considerations]

## Smell

Cats map the home with smell and with pheromones from facial rubbing and scratching. Avoid cleaners and scented products that erase that map. Do not wash every bed at once. Leave facial-marking spots alone in the home. A new item can be rubbed with a cloth that already smells of the cat.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 5]

The 2013 guidelines list a synthetic feline pheromone among methods to reduce anxiety and to support grooming, interest in food, and litter box use. They cite earlier work for that use. This page does not name a brand, and it does not say a diffuser replaces a hiding place or a clean box.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 5]

## A room that already works

- At least one hiding place per cat, easy to enter, and not blocked by another cat.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 1]
- Food, water, toilet, scratch, and rest in separate places, with a second option for rest, food, and toilet.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 2]
- A scratching post that is tall enough to stretch on, in place before the cat arrives.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Training kittens in preparation to be adult cats]
- Daily play with a toy, not with hands.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 3]
- Contact the cat starts and can end.[^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 4]
- No punishment for toileting, marking, or scratching. Change the setup, and call the clinic if the behavior is new.[^aaha-aafp-life-2021|Table 2][^aafp-isfm-env-2013|Pillar 5]
