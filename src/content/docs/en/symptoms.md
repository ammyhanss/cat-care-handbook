This index points from signs an owner can see to pages that exist on this site. A match is not a diagnosis. One sign has many causes. Pages marked draft are outlines only.

If you are unsure, search from the home page, or go to [See a vet now](./urgent/).

## Drooling, bad breath, pain while eating

This can relate to [chronic gingivostomatitis](./diseases/oral/fcgs/) or [tooth resorption](./diseases/oral/tooth-resorption/). It also occurs with oral tumors, foreign bodies, and the breath odor of kidney disease. If the cat has eaten almost nothing for about a day, seek care the same day.

## Almost no food for about a day

Do not spend another day trying a new diet. See [See a vet now](./urgent/). Mouth pain, [FIP](./diseases/infectious/fip/), kidney disease, and diabetic ketoacidosis are among the reasons a cat stops eating.

## Weight loss

Weight loss with a rising appetite raises [hyperthyroidism](./diseases/endocrine/hyperthyroidism/), [diabetes mellitus](./diseases/endocrine/diabetes/), and intestinal disease. Weight loss with a falling appetite is broader and includes [chronic kidney disease](./diseases/urinary/ckd/), [FIP](./diseases/infectious/fip/), and mouth pain. A draft page cannot tell you which one your cat has.

## Drinking more and urinating more

This is common in [chronic kidney disease](./diseases/urinary/ckd/), [diabetes mellitus](./diseases/endocrine/diabetes/), and [hyperthyroidism](./diseases/endocrine/hyperthyroidism/). It needs blood and urine tests. Water intake alone is not a diagnosis. If urine suddenly stops, use [See a vet now](./urgent/).

## Straining, blood in the urine, frequent litter-box trips

See [FLUTD and urethral obstruction](./diseases/urinary/flutd/) and [See a vet now](./urgent/). A male cat that cannot pass urine is an emergency.

## Vomiting

One vomit, with normal energy and appetite afterward, is still worth a phone call. Repeated vomiting, weakness, or a swollen or painful belly should not wait. Ongoing vomiting can occur with kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, diabetes, and intestinal disease.

## Sneezing, nasal discharge, eye discharge

See [upper respiratory infection](./diseases/respiratory/uri/). Open-mouth breathing, labored breathing at rest, or a complete refusal to eat belongs on [See a vet now](./urgent/).

## A larger belly, or faster breathing

Fluid in the abdomen or chest can be a sign of [FIP](./diseases/infectious/fip/). It can also come from heart disease, liver disease, bleeding, or other causes. If breathing is fast or hard, do not try to drain fluid at home. Go to emergency care.

## Fever and marked lethargy

Fever is not a disease. In a young cat, a multi-cat home, a fever that persists, fluid accumulation, or eye changes, a veterinarian will consider [FIP](./diseases/infectious/fip/). Finding coronavirus in blood does not by itself diagnose FIP.

## A change in eye color, or sudden poor vision

This can relate to uveitis from [FIP](./diseases/infectious/fip/), high blood pressure, a corneal ulcer, or upper-respiratory viruses. A suddenly painful, closed, or cloudy eye needs a prompt visit. Do not use human eye drops.

## Wobbly walking or seizures

See [See a vet now](./urgent/) and [FIP](./diseases/infectious/fip/). Toxins, high blood pressure, low blood sugar, and other neurologic problems can look similar.

## Sudden hind-limb paralysis

See [See a vet now](./urgent/). This is not a sign to watch until the next day.

## Possible lily or poison exposure

See [See a vet now](./urgent/) and [Toxicology and emergency](./diseases/toxicology/). Do not wait for signs before you call.
